# The Tricycle at Night

   *1998*

The factory gates closed very decisively. Without much explanation, just a paper notice, we people were moved out of our original lives. After that, I went to pedal a manual tricycle.

The tricycle was rented, with a dark purple cloth canopy and a very heavy iron frame. Every day at four or five in the morning, I had to push the tricycle out of the alley. My movements had to be light, afraid of waking the neighbors. At that time, we still lived in tube-shaped apartment buildings (tongzilou) with thin walls and many people, and no family was well-off.

Pedaling a tricycle has rules. The Transportation Bureau stipulated that odd-numbered tricycles run on odd days, and even-numbered tricycles run on even days. The official explanation was for "fairness," letting everyone have food to eat. But the reality is, the stomach doesn't recognize this rule. The wife needs to buy groceries, the children need to pay miscellaneous fees at school; odd and even days cannot help you hold out for an extra day.

On the days when it wasn't my turn to take the tricycle out, I could only wait until dark. Wait until those Transportation Bureau people got off work, and then push the tricycle out. Business wasn't good at night; sometimes I couldn't even pull two trips in one night, and I also had to watch out for patrols at all times. Once caught, the fine was a hundred yuan.

I remember very clearly, one night when I was waiting for jobs by the roadside, a family of five came over. Two adults and three children. They took a look at the tricycle, then looked at me, didn't say anything more, and started to squeeze on. The children squatted on the footboard, the adults sat with their legs spread, and the tricycle was exactly full. I knew very well in my heart that this wasn't according to the rules and wasn't safe, but I didn't utter a sound.

At a time like that, it's very hard for you to refuse. You know this is overloading, but you also know this might be the only order tonight.

When starting off, I almost couldn't pedal the foot pedals. I practically stood up from the seat, leaned forward desperately, my arms gripping the handlebars tightly, and every muscle in my body tense. The wheels wobbled a few times on the ground before they started moving. By the roadside, some people looked, some averted their eyes. At that time, I was only thinking about one thing: don't stop. If I stopped, I wouldn't be able to pedal it again.

Later, I pulled a pair of brothers. They spoke very politely and asked if I could earn five hundred yuan a month. I didn't answer directly. The number of five hundred yuan was still possible to reach under the conditions of not getting sick, not getting fined, no rain, no snow, and having customers every day. I also didn't say that I used to work in a factory; I was afraid of mentioning the word "laid off."

When they got off, they left without even asking for change. I said thank you several times from behind. I knew that it wasn't because I pulled the tricycle well, but because they felt pity for me.

However, a thing like sympathy isn't always a good thing for people like us. If you don't take the tricycle because you feel "can't bear to" or "embarrassed," it actually makes me feel even worse. We sell our physical strength, not our emotions. If you don't sit, I don't get this order.

Tomorrow it's my turn to take the tricycle out again. I have to get up earlier and push the tricycle out before dawn.

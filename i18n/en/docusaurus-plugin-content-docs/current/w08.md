# Blue Stamp

   *1996*

When I graduated from middle school, it happened to be the years of deepening reform and opening up. The rural contract system had stabilized, country markets became active again, and individual businesses became more and more common. Peasant families like ours no longer relied entirely on farming to make a living.

It was only logical that I took over my father's butchery trade. Buying and slaughtering pigs was considered a proper business in the township. During those years, the town encouraged "getting rich through hard work" and even awarded me the title of "Model of Getting Rich." My photo was published once in the township's small newspaper, which was considered quite a moment of glory. Doing business is all about reputation. I never forced prices down when buying pigs, nor did I give short weight when selling meat, let alone doing things like injecting water or adulterating it. The folks trusted me.

Who would have thought that I would eventually stumble over a stamp.

It was a week before National Day. Meat sold well before the holiday, so I slaughtered a few fat pigs before dawn. According to the rules at the time, live pigs had to go through quarantine before going on the market. The town's epidemic prevention station sent people to the site to inspect, and the passed pig carcasses had to be stamped with a blue quarantine stamp before they could enter the market.

The person who came that day was Officer Li from the epidemic prevention station. He cast a few glances, symbolically flipped through the internal organs, and then took out his stamp and pressed it on the pig carcasses a few times. The blue ink pad was a bit dry, and the imprint was blurry; only a border could be seen.

I bent over with a smile: "Officer Li, please stamp it more clearly. The mark is too faint, it will be hard to explain when I get to the market."

With a cigarette dangling from his mouth, he waved his hand impatiently: "Passed is passed. I'm here in person, how could it be fake? Don't dawdle, I'm in a hurry."

I thought to myself, he is a public official and had collected the quarantine fee, so there shouldn't be any mistakes, and I didn't insist further. I loaded the meat onto the tricycle and hurried to the market.

Inspections were stricter then than before, and private slaughtering and unauthorized selling would be fined. As soon as the stall was set up, business started booming. Regular customers chatted while I weighed the meat, and everything was the same as usual.

But something went wrong that very day.

After Old Zhang's son went back and ate the meat, he started vomiting and having diarrhea. The family got anxious and reported it to the police station. Before long, law enforcement officers came to the market to inspect. They immediately zeroed in on the pork with the blurry stamp. The blue stamp was unclear, so they determined on the spot that the quarantine procedures were questionable and suspected it was privately slaughtered.

I hurriedly went to find Officer Li to testify. However, he turned hostile: "The stamps we use at our station are always very clear. Where is the passing stamp on this meat? I never quarantined this pig!"

He spoke crisp and clear. I stood there stunned, unable to say a single word in reply.

My agricultural tricycle was confiscated on the spot, and the meat was sealed. In the end, it was determined as "selling meat products without valid quarantine," and I was fined five thousand yuan.

A pig only sold for seven or eight hundred yuan; five thousand yuan was almost like the sky falling down. Later I heard that Old Zhang's child just had acute gastroenteritis, which might not necessarily have had anything to do with my meat.

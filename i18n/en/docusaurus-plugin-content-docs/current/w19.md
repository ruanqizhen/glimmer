# Moth

*2006* 

## Dormancy

In the summer of 2000, the echoes of the millennium bell still resonated, and the air was filled with restlessness and hope. Gripping my graduation certificate from the Radio Engineering Department of Southeast University and carrying a tattered backpack, I plunged headfirst into the rolling mountains of southwest China.

To the outside world, the Qixi Space Center was just a blurry coordinate on a map. But in the eyes of us science and engineering geeks, it was a holy land. The base was wrapped in layers of primeval forest, shrouded in clouds and mist year-round. At dawn, when the moist mountain wind dispersed the miasma at the bottom of the valley, the towering command building would pierce the clouds like a silver sword pointing straight to the sky. From afar, those steel behemoths looked like a floating castle in the clouds, carrying a mystery and coldness that didn't belong to this world.

I was assigned to the Fourth Research Office, specializing in quantum communication modules. We were trying to build an absolutely secure encryption system within the entanglement of photons. In this isolated mountain valley, my companions were the eerie green glow of oscilloscopes, the endless low-frequency hum of the server room, and a goal only we could understand: to make interstellar communication unmonitorable by any enemy nation.

It was a crazy era.

The superpower across the ocean had just high-profilely announced the "Mars Colonization Plan," boasting that they would make Olympus Mons a colony of the Western Empire within ten years. This news was like a depth charge, stirring up massive waves in the international community. In response, the Qixi Space Center quickly launched a grand blueprint codenamed "Pillar of Heaven". Our goal was clear: to be the first to build a human outpost on that desolate red planet.

The entire base entered an unprecedented state of hyper-excitement. The roar of rocket engine test runs would tremble the valley almost every day, and the launch schedule was as densely packed as a complex integrated circuit board. This crazy expansion directly led to a massive shortage of astronauts. Especially technical personnel who could handle complex data and debug equipment on-site became resources rarer than pandas.

That was 2002. To fill the gap, the space center unprecedentedly opened the selection channel to internal researchers. People privately joked that this was a "massive watering down" of standards, but I knew that this might be my only chance in this lifetime to touch the stars. Relying on my hardcore skills in radio and quantum communication, along with the physical stamina I built up playing soccer in college, I fought my way through the narrow gates of rigorous screening like a fish swimming upstream.

On the twilight I officially transferred to the Astronaut Brigade, I stood under the massive glass dome of the training center, gazing up at the star map dotted on the skylight. My heart was filled with the excitement of an imminent dream coming true, mixed with apprehension for the unknown journey. Outside the dome, the afterglow of the setting sun dyed the western sky a brilliant orange-red, and the distant mountains gradually faded into the twilight, as if the scroll of my beautiful future was slowly unfolding.

However, fate excels at suddenly snapping the strings just when the symphony reaches its highest note.

Less than half a year after I joined the astronaut brigade, a global financial tsunami erupted without warning. The collapse of Wall Street triggered a domino effect; energy prices skyrocketed, and unemployment soared. To stop the bleeding of economies on the brink of collapse, governments ruthlessly slashed those high-investment, long-cycle research projects. Aerospace, once seen as the beacon of humanity's future, instantly became the most glaring burden on the budget sheets. Winter came exceptionally early to Qixi. All extraterrestrial exploration plans were urgently halted, and the "Pillar of Heaven" project was indefinitely sealed. The once-bustling launch site became deathly silent, with only wild grass growing wildly beneath the rust-stained towers. The funding cuts even affected daily training; the food in the brigade's cafeteria grew blander by the day.

Teammates began to look for ways out. Some used connections to transfer to lucrative administrative departments; others reluctantly took off their uniforms and jumped ship to coastal telecom giants, which were among the few industries still making money at the time. Farewell drinking parties happened one after another, and the former heroic declarations all turned into helpless sighs amidst the clinking of glasses.

And I became the shell left behind on the beach. My original R&D department was overstaffed and underfunded, so I couldn't go back; leaving Qixi left me unwilling. Thus, I became an awkwardly "left-behind astronaut," drawing a meager allowance, living as an idle person with nothing to do in this mountain base that was gradually being forgotten.

During that time, I often went to the empty training center alone, sitting silently in the cold simulator, staring blankly through the screen at the virtual starry sky. Below the stars, the red surface of Mars flickered with a faint, mysterious light under the sun, as if silently calling to me. I knew I was very much like a moth lurking in the dark; the fire of my desire for space had not extinguished, it was merely covered by the ice and snow of reality. I was waiting for a single spark to burst forth from the dead ashes.


## Spinning the Cocoon

In early 2006, the long-silent Qixi Space Center suddenly sprang back to life.

That morning, after the morning exercise ended, the cold wind was piercing. The instructor stood with his hands behind his back, his gaze sweeping over our group of idle men with red, frozen noses, and announced: The space center would urgently recruit three "Special Operations Team Members" for the next space mission. The instructor demanded that everyone actively sign up for the selection tests, and he specifically emphasized that this was the first major space mission initiated by the Qixi Space Center in four years.

Although the instructor was vague, who among us wasn't shrewd? Everyone knew that this mission was very likely the restart of the "Pillar of Heaven" project—the construction of a human outpost, the ultimate glory dreamed of by all astronauts.

As soon as the instructor said the word "dismissed," the training ground exploded with chatter. The few of us jogged all the way back to our dorm.

"Bullshit!" Guofeng threw his hat on the bed and plopped down on the creaky iron frame, looking disdainful. "Could such a good thing fall on our laps? It's been rigged from the start! Look at that special-class hero Chen Weimin, and the guys under him—they've probably already drank the celebration wine. We're just going to play extras, keeping the crown prince company."

Xinyu, who was wiping his sweat, echoed, "There are only three spots. I just heard from the guys in Squad 5 that the selection standards are ridiculously high this time. It's obviously tailor-made for certain people."

Wenhua, who had always been a man of few words, sighed and slowly folded his quilt. "There's no harm in it. For guys with shallow qualifications like us, getting a participation award will make our resumes look a bit better."

They were all telling the truth. As a group of "left-behind personnel" who had been marginalized for three years, we had long been accustomed to playing supporting roles. But deep down, I still held on to a faint glimmer of hope. The me who looked up at the stars alone in the simulator every night, the boy who plastered his walls with notebooks drawn full of rockets twenty years ago, started shouting in my heart again. Even if it was just running alongside, I wanted to be closer to that track.

"So are you guys signing up?" I asked tentatively.

Guofeng rolled his eyes and pulled out the registration form he had just received. "Of course! Why not? What if a blind cat catches a dead mouse?"

Xinyu and Wenhua also smiled and nodded in agreement.


Signing up was just the beginning; what followed was three days of hellish testing.

It was torture designed to break through human physiological and psychological defenses. On the first day's centrifuge test, the G-force was cranked straight to 8G. I still remember the feeling—as if my eyeballs were being crushed into my brain, my vision rapidly narrowing into a black tunnel, the air in my lungs forcibly squeezed out, and every breath feeling like swallowing shattered glass.

Right after being freed from the spinning nightmare, before even throwing up all the sour water in our stomachs, we were stuffed into the hypobaric chamber.

The air pressure dropped to a third of sea level. Hypoxia made my fingertips tingle, and my brain felt like an old computer with a broken cooling fan, running sluggishly. But I didn't dare stop. Although my teammates with fighter pilot backgrounds also showed pain, they could still complete standard tactical maneuvers. Meanwhile, someone from an academic background like me could only rely on sheer willpower to hold on.

"Ruan Qizhen, heart rate too fast. Abort?" The monitor's cold inquiry came through the earpiece.

"Continue." I bit my lip, using the pain to stimulate my fading consciousness.

The second day was the profoundly oppressive deep space psychological endurance test. We were locked individually in narrow, enclosed simulators without any windows, only cold, hard metal walls. The test required us to stay in a completely isolated, dark environment for a full 24 hours, without eating or drinking, while completing a series of complex tasks in a highly stressed state. This sense of isolation and the oppressive darkness constantly pounded my nerves, testing my psychological endurance. I tried my best to control my emotions and keep myself from breaking down. Ultimately, my stress resistance was evaluated as "barely passing," a result that left me feeling somewhat frustrated. After the test ended, I walked out of the simulator to find my back completely soaked in cold sweat; I was on the verge of collapse.

The final day was a high-intensity comprehensive test conducted in a state of extreme exhaustion after over 24 hours without sleep. It included written exams, high-difficulty spacecraft piloting simulations, emergency troubleshooting, and assessments of field survival skills and geological surveying knowledge. My head felt dizzy, my limbs incredibly heavy, and I could barely focus. My overall performance was mediocre; I only managed to save some face in the technical theory exams, scoring an acceptable grade.

Returning to the dorm, I collapsed on the bed like a puddle of mud. Guofeng and the others were still enthusiastically reviewing their operational mistakes. All three of them had been fighter pilots before joining the astronaut brigade; these tests were a piece of cake for them. But I didn't even have the strength to move a finger. I knew I had no chance. Compared to their robust physiques, my only advantage was those dry codes and formulas. And in an astronaut brigade that worshipped strength and courage, that was worthless.


Fate is equally adept at playing the most soaring movements on broken strings.

Early the next morning, right after the wake-up bugle, the radio suddenly crackled with the voice of Director Wang from the training center, deep and devoid of any emotion: "Ruan Qizhen, come to my office immediately."

My heart sank, and a strange unease welled up inside me. What could the director want with me so early? Without time to think, I hurriedly threw on my uniform and jogged all the way to Director Wang's office.

He was alone in the office, a thick stack of documents piled on his desk. The light shone on his face, making him look somewhat gloomy.

"Reporting!"

He looked up, his gaze lingering on me for a few seconds. It was a strange look—not like looking at a subordinate, but rather sizing up a part about to be scrapped that still had to be used.

"Sit." He pointed to a chair, skipping the pleasantries, and pushed a document directly in front of me. "Ruan Qizhen, after a comprehensive evaluation, you have been selected."

I was stunned, my mind went blank, thinking I had misheard. "Director, you mean... I've been selected for 'Pillar of Heaven V'?"

"Yes." Director Wang's voice remained flat, even seeming a bit tired. "Although your physical fitness is a shortcoming, your professional background in quantum communication is urgently needed for this mission. There is some equipment that only you can repair. This is the letter of appointment. Sign it, and report to the boot camp immediately."

I doubted whether I heard it right or if I was still dreaming. It took a few seconds before I barely recovered my senses, my trembling hands accepting the heavy document. Printed on the cover in bright red letters was "Top Secret." Flipping to the first page, my name and the mission code—"Pillar of Heaven V Mars Exploration Mission"—leaped into my eyes.

At that moment, an indescribable ecstasy instantly surged into my heart, as if an electric current shot straight from the soles of my feet to the top of my head, filling my entire body with strength. I felt light, as if I were going to fly. Suppressing the urge to shout in triumph, I signed my name on the appointment letter with a trembling hand.

"Go." Director Wang waved his hand, seemingly unwilling to spare me another glance. "Remember, confidentiality regulations. Before the official launch, you are not allowed to disclose specific mission details to anyone, including your teammates and family."

I practically ran out of the office, afraid this sudden good luck would disappear. In the hallway, I almost bumped into a few team members coming my way, but they seemed busy with their own affairs, and no one noticed the uncontrollable excitement on my face.


Returning to the dorm, I immediately started frantically packing my belongings, stuffing everything into my duffel bag. At this time, Guofeng and the others had just returned from their morning run. Seeing me in this state, their eyes widened, and they asked in confusion, "Qizhen, are you absconding?"

Xinyu also came over, asking curiously, "Yeah, what did Director Wang call you for?"

I tried my best to suppress my inner excitement, pretending to look helpless. "Don't bring it up. I've been drafted. The launch site in Xinjiang had a little accident; the technicians there can't handle it and insist I go over to help investigate. I'll probably be gone for a while."

Hearing this, Guofeng's originally teasing expression instantly turned serious. He walked up to me, frowning: "Xinjiang? I heard a rocket exploded there a few days ago, and the propellant leak is pretty severe. You gotta be careful when you get there; that toxic fuel is no joke."

Looking at his sincere, caring eyes, a trace of guilt rose in my heart. But I had to continue weaving the lie: "Don't worry, I'm just in charge of looking at data, I won't go to the site. As for you guys, I won't be holding you back in morning exercises anymore, so make sure you bring back some 'Excellent' grades."

Wenhua also gathered around, lowering his voice: "Rumor has it the explosion was caused by anti-government armed forces. It's not peaceful over there."

Guofeng sneered, a sharpness rarely seen by others flashing in his eyes: "Anti-government armed forces? Pushing all the blame onto them is the easiest way out. These bloodsucking bureaucrats will drive the whole country to ruin sooner or later." He paused, helped me adjust my backpack, and said earnestly, "The water is deep there, keep an eye out for yourself. When you come back, let's go eat Old Liu's iron pot stewed goose."

"Absolutely."

I shouldered my duffel bag and took one last look at this cramped dorm room I had lived in for four years, and at these brothers who were like family to me. When I return triumphant from Mars, I will slap my medals on the table and treat them to the strongest liquor.

I waved my hand and walked out the door without looking back.


## Pupation

Standing before the towering iron gates of the boot camp, I took a deep breath of the early spring chill and looked back at the road I had come from. I knew that once I stepped through this gate, I would be temporarily cut off from the outside world; for the next two years, I would probably not be able to contact my family and friends at all. From this moment on, the trajectory of my life would completely change, and my life would turn to a brand-new page. And my gaze would be cast towards the red wasteland hundreds of millions of kilometers away.

This Mars mission selected a total of six astronauts, divided into Groups A and B, three people each.

Group A was the darling of the spotlight. The captain was the special-class space hero Chen Weimin, whose resolute square face dominated the headlines of major media outlets year-round. Team members Li Wei and Zhang Hao were also elite graduates of top universities and ace pilots. As they walked around the base, they looked like a flock of proud swans; even the cut of their uniforms seemed to fit them better and crisper than ours.

In contrast, our Group B was practically a "motley crew." Apart from Captain Zhao Yongjun—an experienced but outspoken veteran astronaut who had been benched for years for offending leaders—the remaining Ma Xiaomei and I were technicians-turned-astronauts, rookies who had never been on a mission before.

At first, this obvious personnel arrangement made me mutter to myself: Are we just spares?

But the subsequent training schedule dispelled my doubts. Group A's training focused on command, scientific research, and large machinery operation; they sat in climate-controlled simulation cabins, elegantly pushing and pulling joysticks. Meanwhile, our Group B trained exclusively on tedious physical labor: wearing bulky spacesuits, assembling structures outside the cabin, repairing malfunctions, etc. Both groups had to cooperate to complete the Mars base construction; we were not substitutes for one another.


In the tactical briefing room, a holographic projection revealed the true face of "Pillar of Heaven V" for the first time. At that moment, even a tech-oriented guy like me couldn't help but hold my breath.

This was a mobile fortress capable of navigating the sea of stars, composed of three parts:

At the center was the main ship, "Spark". It was like a luxurious space palace, equipped with the most advanced closed-loop ecological cycle system, a spacious scientific research cabin, and highly private living quarters. That would be our warm home during the long journey.

Topped at the front of the main ship was the cargo bay, "Stowage," a massive, silent space container whose belly was stuffed with the special building materials and construction robots needed to build the Mars outpost.

Hanging beneath the main ship was the "Stardust" landing craft, responsible for ferrying. This spacecraft contrasted sharply with the glamorous "Spark." It used an old design finalized decades ago; although durable and sturdy, its interior space was cramped, and the densely packed instrument panels exuded a last-century industrial vibe.

"Why use such an old model?" Xiao Ma had complained privately.

"Because it's reliable." The instructor's answer was impeccable. "In the harsh environment of Mars, the more primitive the mechanical structure, the less likely it is to malfunction."

In terms of power systems, "Spark" was the pinnacle of human engineering. Its heart was a QSL3 small nuclear fusion reactor, providing an endless stream of energy to the 200-megawatt "Galaxy Pulse Type II" ion thruster at its tail. This eerie blue engine would propel us back and forth between Earth and Mars.

However, the ion engine's thrust was weak and could not lift off from the atmosphere. Therefore, the heavy responsibility of ascending was handed over to the "Storm Type III" heavy launch vehicle. It would carry the spacecraft along Qixi's world-famous ultra-long maglev track, first being electromagnetically accelerated to Mach 3, then igniting in mid-air, piercing the sky like a cloud-piercing arrow.

According to the plan, upon reaching Mars orbit, "Spark" would hover, and "Stardust" would act like a diligent worker bee, traveling back and forth between orbit and the surface multiple times to deliver personnel and supplies in batches. Once the base was built, this hard-working "worker bee" and the "Stowage" would be discarded, and all of us would ride "Spark" home in glory.

The entire mission was expected to take fourteen months.


The days at boot camp flew by, filled with high-intensity simulation training every day. Although Groups A and B were on the same base, we were completely separated by the simulation cabins. Aside from occasionally nodding at each other in the cafeteria, we had almost no interaction.

It wasn't until the thirtieth day of boot camp, our only day of rest, that we faced a special "mission": a camera crew from CCTV entered the base.

It was my first time coming into close contact with the entertainment industry. Even though I was facing the dream girl of countless tech geeks—famous host Bingbing—I couldn't get excited at all. Because in the makeup room, a clerk from the propaganda department shoved a thick script into my hands and said coldly, "Memorize it, and don't change a single word."

And so, under the spotlight, I was forced to put on a fake mask.

My name wasn't Ruan Qizhen; I was a "Model Hero." Facing Bingbing's eyes, which seemed affectionate yet highly professional, I mechanically recounted the story fabricated by the propaganda department: "How I aspired to spaceflight since childhood, how I burned down my house doing experiments. Although my father beat me, that fire ignited my aerospace dream..." As I spoke the insincere lines, I thought to myself, at least getting beaten was true.

Even our personalities were engineered. Captain Chen Weimin was the "anchor," I was the "tech-obsessed geek," and Zhang Hao was the "hot-blooded tough guy," and so on. We were like carefully painted dolls placed on a shelf, showing the public the flawless perfection of national will.

At the end of the interview, Bingbing politely shook my hand and said, "Wishing you a triumphant return. When the program airs, you should already be nearing the moon, and the people of the whole country will be watching you then."

Her hand was very soft and cold—no wonder she was called Bingbing (Ice).

Looking at her bright smile, an inexplicable sense of anticipation welled up in my heart. I imagined myself watching the replay of this interview in space, imagining my fellow townsmen pointing at the TV and praising me.


## Emerging from the Chrysalis

Two months slipped by like fine sand in an hourglass before I knew it. The day of departure had arrived.

In the early morning, the Qixi Space Center was shrouded in a thin morning mist. The first rays of sunlight pierced the clouds, shining on the twenty-kilometer maglev track. That silver line shooting straight up into the clouds along the Xuanlong Ridge looked like a stairway to heaven.

"Pillar of Heaven V" lay quietly at the starting line of the track. Its silver-white alloy shell reflected cold, flowing light in the morning sun, resembling a temple from the future. Wearing my spacesuit, I stood at the entrance of the boarding bridge, my heart pounding violently. This was the first time I had looked up at this steel behemoth from so close; every rivet and weld exuded a beauty that suffocated any engineering nerd.

I took a deep breath and stepped toward the spacious airlock of the main ship "Spark." However, a hand wearing a white glove barred my way.

"Ruan Qizhen, wait a moment."

The person stopping me was a ground crew supervisor, expressionless, looking at me as if I were a piece of incorrectly labeled cargo. He pointed to a dim side passage below the bridge tower: "Received a temporary order. During the ascent phase, all members of Group B will ride in the 'Stardust' landing craft."

"What?" I froze. "Why wasn't this notified beforehand? Changing the plan now? Why?"

"Weight distribution adjustment," the supervisor spat out coldly, with no intention of explaining, barely even looking at me. "It's an order."

Suppressing the doubts in my heart, I turned and walked through the narrow passage toward the side door. "Stardust" was designed for short-distance transfers; its interior space was cramped, like a forcibly stuffed sardine can.



Among Group B, I was the last to board. Old Zhao sat by the window, checking his safety harness, his brows locked tightly together.

Xiao Ma was checking the control panel, muttering to herself. Seeing me enter, she punched the armrest and said, "What is this? The lords in Group A get to ride in first class, and we have to squeeze into the cargo hold? Even if it's for weight distribution, isn't this too insulting?"

"Say less," Old Zhao immediately stopped her, sensing the mood was wrong. "Since we're here, let's settle down. As long as we complete the mission, it doesn't matter where we sit." But his gaze remained fixed outside the window on the umbilical cable disconnecting from the main ship, his eyes dark and unreadable. "When we get to Mars, the tasks we execute are the most important. Everything will become clear then."

I squeezed into the corner seat and fastened my seatbelt. The confined space was suffocating, and an inexplicable unease grew wild like weeds in my heart.


At exactly 9:00 AM, the countdown reached zero.

There was none of the deafening roar of a traditional rocket ignition; instead, it was replaced by a deep, dense electrical hum and the sound of air friction.

"Track charging complete, electromagnetic catapult initiated!"

A massive thrust hit instantly—not the feeling of being kicked in the back, but rather as if an invisible giant hand was pinning my entire body firmly against the seatback. The scenery outside the window began to retreat rapidly. At first, the supports of the launch tower were clear, but just seconds later, everything stretched into blurry blocks of color.

The speed climbed continuously. Mach 1, Mach 2...

The landing craft sped along the maglev track, the vibrations shooting up my spine straight to the top of my skull. I felt as if my internal organs were being squeezed into a ball, and breathing became incredibly difficult.

In less than a minute, with a sharp metallic boom, the earthly constraints vanished instantly. The spacecraft shot out from the end of the track like a stone hurled high into the sky by immense force. After a split second of heart-stopping weightlessness, the main engines of the Storm Type III rocket ignited.

It was as if someone had detonated a nuclear bomb behind us. The massive roar instantly drowned out all hearing, and terrifying G-forces struck like a collapsing mountain. I felt like my eyeballs were going to explode, and my vision quickly narrowed into a black tunnel. We were riding on the back of a fire-breathing dragon, being pushed into the dark cosmos with the most violent force I had ever known.

The intense shaking lasted for ten whole minutes, each second feeling like a century.

Finally, with a slight jolt, the roar abruptly stopped. The pain of the seatbelts digging into our bodies vanished, replaced by a weightless sensation so light it felt as if my soul had left my body.


The blue Earth hovered in the pitch-black space, breathtakingly beautiful. The wreckage of the "Storm Type III" rocket streaked across the sky like a meteor, eventually pulled back to Earth by gravity, disappearing into the vast edge of the world. Xiao Ma looked at this scene, her eyes slightly red: "This life was worth it."

At this time, the ion engine of the main ship "Spark" activated. An eerie blue beam pierced the darkness, propelling us to continue accelerating. In the low-gravity environment, the atmosphere inside the cabin suddenly eased a lot. Although cramped, we were in space, after all.

Captain Chen's orders came through the radio, instructing Group B members to remain in the landing craft and await further instructions.

I checked all the communication facilities in the landing craft. I looked at the flashing red light next to the console; that was the interface for the redundant communication module. As its designer, I knew there was a backdoor left inside for "debugging convenience."

"Comrades," I suddenly spoke up, breaking the silence, "Want to see how the news is blowing smoke about us? I can connect this thing to the geosynchronous satellite's TV signal."

To ensure the absolute security of interstellar navigation and to prevent any potential information leaks, the communication system of the entire "Pillar of Heaven V" starship was strictly restricted. According to the space center's strict regulations, the spacecraft could only communicate with the ground command center via a quantum-encrypted dedicated channel; any communication modules supporting public protocols were prohibited. This was to minimize the risk of external cyberattacks and information leaks. However, to deal with possible emergencies—such as the landing craft unexpectedly taking damage or crash-landing—the designers still installed a one-way TV and radio signal receiving module inside the "Stardust" landing craft. This module was usually completely shut down, only automatically activating when specific emergency signals or commands were detected. But as the designer, I naturally knew the method to turn it on manually.


This was undoubtedly a violation of regulations, but under the resentment of being treated differently, it felt more like a harmless revenge. Everyone felt that this small "rule-breaking behavior" was both thrilling and satisfying, seemingly bringing us a temporary sense of vindication.

"Connect it!" Xiao Ma rubbed her hands excitedly. "I want to see my parents crying in front of the TV."

Old Zhao pretended not to hear, tacitly consenting.

The originally somewhat dull and cramped cabin suddenly became lively.

I unfastened my seatbelt, carefully floated over to the control panel, and began operating the signal receiver. I bypassed the main ship's security protocols and forced the backup high-gain antenna to aim at the "Hongyin" broadcasting satellite in geosynchronous orbit. A few seconds later, the communication screen lit up, the static gradually cleared, and sharp news footage popped out. It was CCTV's live special program: "Journey: Humanity Returns to Mars."

The three of us huddled our heads together, holding our breath.

Major media outlets nationwide were giving overwhelming coverage to the launch of "Pillar of Heaven V," and major TV stations were broadcasting the whole event live. After the spacecraft entered its intended orbit, the news channel began repeatedly analyzing the details of the mission, conducting comprehensive propaganda on this highly significant interstellar exploration. After all, this was humanity's attempt to establish a permanent outpost on Mars after many years, and its historical and symbolic significance was extraordinary. The three of us were full of anticipation, hoping to show the world the elegance of astronauts through the reports.

On the CCTV screen, the host was passionately introducing the space heroes. A large photo of Chen Weimin occupied the entire screen, followed by Li Wei and Zhang Hao. Their biographies, hobbies, and even elementary school awards were dug up clearly. During this time, they cut to a live broadcast from inside "Spark," where the astronauts responded to the leader's greetings and paid their respects to the people of the whole country. "Stardust," however, was completely ignored.

Finally, the screen cut back to the studio. "Let us once again pay tribute to these three warriors!" the host said emotionally. "Over the next year, they will represent all of humanity, exploring and building this red wasteland alone..."

Until the program ended, the host always used "three people" to introduce the mission members. They even mentioned the humanoid robots that would participate in the construction of the Mars base, but they didn't mention any Group B members at all. It was as if we were never part of the mission. We were like ghosts, silently melting into the cold vacuum.

"What... what does this mean?" I was somewhat bewildered. "Live broadcast delay? Or confidentiality agreement?"

"Something's not right," Xiao Ma quickly interjected. "The leadership never said our confidentiality levels were different. Why can Group A be public while we must be kept secret?"


Old Zhao seemed to suddenly think of something, turning his head sharply, his tone suddenly severe: "Forget the news!"

His gaze swept over us, rapidly issuing commands: "Little Ruan, you try receiving foreign media signals and see how they report it. Xiao Ma, you and I will go over all mission data again and compare it with the simulation training to see if there are any changes."

He was the most experienced astronaut among the three of us, the backbone of the team. Nearing retirement age, he still refused to concede to old age and insisted on participating in this mission.

Xiao Ma jolted at the shout and immediately lunged at the terminal: "Stardust's mainframe has backups of all important files. I'll look for the external propaganda materials; what the official statements say should give us a clue." As she spoke, her fingers were already flying across the screen.

A few minutes later, she found several unencrypted propaganda documents and quickly projected them onto the main screen in the cabin. The screen popped up an eye-catching title: "Pillar of Heaven V Exploration Mission: Three-Person Team." The document detailed the basic mission information, as well as the selection process and personal profiles of the three astronauts in Group A: photos, names, resumes, division of labor—everything was there.

But from beginning to end, not a single word was mentioned about the three of us in Group B. It was as if we simply didn't exist. This completely contradicted what had been announced previously.

In the narrow landing craft, the air seemed to freeze instantly. Only the life support system emitted a monotonous buzzing sound, making one's skin crawl. Just then, Xiao Ma suddenly cried out in a low voice, "Captain..." Cold sweat seeped from her forehead, tiny droplets floating slowly in the microgravity, reflecting the pale light from the panel. "I checked it three times... I found a few stats... they're definitely wrong."

"Read," Old Zhao said, just one word.

"The total payload of the spacecraft is consistent with the simulation phase," Xiao Ma's voice tightened. "But Spark's carrying capacity, food reserves, max load of the water cycle system, number of oxygen regeneration plates, etc., are all significantly lower than the training data."

She paused, as if confirming: "The actual provisions of the spacecraft can at most sustain five people for a year."

No one opened their mouths to ask why.

A suffocating silence fell within the landing craft, leaving only the monotonous and regular hum of the life support system in our ears. That sound no longer seemed like a machine running; it sounded more like the grim reaper impatiently tapping the stopwatch of a countdown.


## Eclosion

The communication indicator light suddenly began flashing crazily, breaking the suffocating silence. Xiao Ma glanced at the console, and her face instantly changed: "It's the ground center! The call code... it's Director Cai!"

Director Cai had been Xiao Ma's old captain, and the two were as close as sisters. But at this moment, the voice coming from the speaker was no longer the steady strong woman we knew; it was filled with urgent and suppressed trembling: "Xiao Ma! Listen carefully. As soon as I finish speaking, immediately cut the main communication! I will use encrypted channel 23 to send you a video. Watch it immediately!"

"Channel 23?" Xiao Ma's hand froze mid-air.

Channel 23 was the highest-level "one-way secure line," which even the IT department of the space center couldn't monitor or crack. According to regulations, unless in extreme emergencies, it was strictly forbidden to activate it privately. And Director Cai, as one of the original system architects, held the highest physical access rights to this channel—this was perhaps the one oversight made by those bureaucrats above.

Xiao Ma muttered to herself, "This doesn't comply with protocol. Should I ask Captain Chen first?"

"Hurry up!" I couldn't help urging. "We don't even know where we're being sent to die, and you're worried about getting a demerit?"

Xiao Ma gritted her teeth and rapidly typed on the keyboard. After a few seconds of electrical static, Director Cai's holographic projection unfolded in the center of the cramped cabin.

She looked terrible. Her usually meticulously combed short hair was somewhat messy, and her eyes were red, as if she had just cried or hadn't slept for days. More terrifying was the fear in her eyes—a fear as if she had looked straight into hell.

"There's not much time, I'll keep it short," Director Cai's voice trembled. "This is a... highest-level top-secret plan, codenamed 'Moth.' I also just found out... I'm sorry, I really... don't know how to begin..."

"Over two months ago, a top-secret intelligence report reached the high command's desk: The Western Empire will use the upcoming Earth-Mars transfer window to advance their Mars base layout. To gain a leading position in this new space race, the higher-ups decided to immediately restart the stalled 'Pillar of Heaven V' mission."

"But... there simply wasn't enough time." Director Cai painfully covered her face. "The original outpost construction plan was designed for six astronauts to cooperate, but spacecraft development has stalled over these years, and the payload capacity hasn't kept up. Current technology cannot return all six people from Mars. 'Spark' is still in the configuration of the previous mission; its life support cycle, fuel reserves, and even return weight distribution are all calculated for three people."

"In such a short time, we didn't have time to redesign the outpost, nor modify Spark, and we couldn't prepare an extra spacecraft. So, they came up with a 'genius' solution, which is the internal plan codenamed 'Moth.'"

Director Cai raised her head, her eyes full of sorrow: "The plan was to select a group of astronauts with mediocre qualifications and low public attention to form Group B, which is you. First, use Group B to complete the most dangerous initial construction of the base, and then... manufacture an 'accident.'"

"To leave enough supplies for Group B to return to Earth, Group B must quietly disappear." Director Cai's voice was so low it was almost inaudible, each word seemingly squeezed through her teeth. "In the eyes of the public and the international community, this mission will be a perfect three-person space exploration, an absolutely flawless heroic feat..."

At this moment, it was so quiet in the cabin that not even breathing could be heard. Anger? Fear? No, what occupied my mind in that instant was a massive, absurd sense of nihilism. We had thought we were warriors setting out for the future of humanity, bearing the heavy trust of the nation. It turned out that in the eyes of those high-and-mighty decision-makers, we were just single-use fuel, a flock of moths that had to be burned to ashes to illuminate someone else's medals.

Even our names didn't deserve to be on the monument.


"To hell with heroes!" I couldn't control my emotions anymore and slammed my fist against the bulkhead. "On what grounds? Why aren't our lives considered lives?!"

Despair spread like a plague; Xiao Ma was already sobbing uncontrollably.

"Shut up, all of you!" Old Zhao stood up abruptly. This veteran, past fifty, had a chilling light burning in his eyes at this moment. It was a look that only those who had truly faced death possessed.

"What's the use of crying?" Old Zhao's voice wasn't loud, but it cracked like a whip across everyone's heart. "Since they treat us like dead men, we're going to live just to show them! Not only will we survive, but we'll also tear the masks off those bastards and show the whole world!"

He turned to look at me, his gaze so heavy that it made me slightly unsteady: "Little Ruan, you've always been responsible for communicating with Group A. You go over to 'Spark' first, communicate with Captain Chen, and tell them the truth. I'll gather some data, and then we'll meet up."

Then, he cast his gaze toward Xiao Ma: "Back up all the documents and recordings Director Cai just sent! Then, we'll reorganize all the materials we just collected."

"Will... will this work?" I hesitated, not leaving immediately. "Group A are the vested interests; from the broadcast just now, they seem... what if..."

"Chen Weimin is a true soldier." Old Zhao said categorically. "He may be arrogant, but he is never despicable. I trust him."


I requested permission from Group A to open the hatch, squeezed through the passage, and floated into the brightly lit "Spark." This was my first time seeing the interior of the real "Spark." It wasn't exactly the same as the simulation cabin; the space was noticeably smaller, barely fitting three operation chairs.

Li Wei and Zhang Hao were busy with their tasks, wearing relaxed and confident expressions on their faces, completely oblivious to my state, much less knowing what kind of hell the "iron can" next door had just experienced.

Captain Chen stood at the hatch waiting for me and asked first: "Little Ruan, I just received an alert saying you guys activated Channel 23. Why didn't you apply in advance?"


I tried to use a calm tone, but still couldn't hide a trace of trembling, whispering: "Captain Chen, I must report something immediately; this concerns the lives of all of us..." My voice was a bit hoarse, but I tried my best to keep my tone steady, not letting myself appear too panicked.

Then, I walked over to the console and projected Director Cai's video onto the large screen.

Hearing our conversation, Li Wei and Zhang Hao also gathered around. At first, they had a trace of nonchalance and confusion on their faces, but as Director Cai's voice sounded, and as documents stamped with top-secret seals unfolded, the air in the cabin seemed to be instantly sucked dry.

Those elites who had just been chatting and laughing had their smiles gradually freeze, then crumble little by little, finally turning deathly pale.

"I think we need to verify the authenticity of the video." Young Li Wei was the first to speak. "How could higher-ups make such a decision? Could the communication system have been hacked by an enemy state?"

I looked at them and said coldly, "The supply manifest is in the system. Calculate it yourselves; how many people is it enough for?"

Zhang Hao volunteered to go to the cargo bay to inspect the supplies. Twenty minutes later, he returned dejectedly, reporting that the cargo bay supplies matched the records—only enough for three people to make the return trip.


Chen Weimin stared deadly at the screen, his chest heaving violently. As a special-class hero, the things he had believed in his entire life were on the verge of collapse at this moment. He had always thought he was fighting for a noble cause, but he never expected to be used as a blade in the hands of executioners.

The cabin fell into a deathly silence. Chen Weimin closed his eyes, his heavy breathing echoing in the empty cabin. On one side was the country he swore loyalty to and absolute obedience to military orders; on the other side were living comrades and the conscience he had held on to for half his life. This desperate silence lasted for two whole minutes; for Li Wei and Zhang Hao, these two minutes were longer than a whole century.

"Bang!"

Chen Weimin abruptly opened his eyes and slammed his fist hard on the titanium alloy desktop; the loud noise made everyone shudder.

"Bastards!"

The tough guy's eyes were red, filled not only with anger but also with the indignation and shame of shattered faith. "What do they take us for? Accomplices? Or fools?!" He turned around, his gaze sweeping over his team members like a torch, his voice carrying unquestionable resolution: "To hell with the political big picture! If we just carry out the order like this, returning home with our brothers' bones to collect medals, are we still human? Does this spacesuit still house a human heart?!"

Li Wei stood behind the captain: "I'd rather die in space than step on my comrades' corpses to go home."

"Me too." Zhang Hao echoed.


Looking at these righteous "chosen ones," a warm current surged in my heart. In this cold, dark universe, although politics are dirty, the faint light of humanity still flickers.

"Little Ruan, go back and notify Old Zhao. We are brothers on the same boat." Chen Weimin took a deep breath, restoring his commander's calm. "I have an important meeting coming up. In half an hour, we'll all assemble to discuss a response plan. Don't worry, all six of us comrades, not a single one will be missing!"



## Moths to the Flame

Half an hour later, in the main control room of Spark, the air was so heavy it felt like water could drip from it. The six people gathered around the holographic star chart, like six gamblers about to bet their entire fortunes.

Captain Chen stood in the center of the main cabin, his expression solemn. His gaze swept over everyone present as he spoke: "We are now a collective; we must be completely honest with each other. I'll first share the information I've learned. Just now, I had an urgent communication with Commander Gao. After understanding the situation I reported, Commander Gao ordered me to adjust course and enter Earth orbit to stand by. Subsequently, I received a formal order from Secretary Yan, requiring us to continue executing the original plan. Following that, the ground command center cut off our communications. Right now, aside from broadcast TV signals, we have completely lost contact with the outside world."

He paused for a moment, seeming to weigh his next words. Everyone in the cabin held their breath, waiting for him to continue.

"One thing is certain: going to Mars is a dead end." Chen Weimin's gaze swept over everyone, carrying a desperate ruthlessness. "I know none of you fear sacrifice. But no one should die an unexplained death; I cannot let us become victims of a political game. Given that the orders from above contradict each other, and we have no way to communicate further, I suggest we return immediately. We cannot sit here and wait for death, waiting for others to dictate our fate. We must go back now, take the initiative to expose the truth, and uphold justice."

"I agree!" Old Zhao was the first to respond. "We cannot become pawns of others, much less be silently abandoned in space!"

Everyone nodded, and each person successively spoke to express their agreement.

"Since everyone is in agreement, Little Li, you are responsible for continuing to try and contact the command center; Little Ruan, Xiao Ma, the two of you are our team's technical experts, responsible for plotting the return course. Old Zhao, Little Zhang, let's discuss our specific response strategies."

Following Captain Chen's orders, the cabin instantly became busy. Everyone knew that the next steps were crucial and would determine all of our fates.

Theoretically speaking, the "Stardust" landing craft was capable of landing in various surface environments, be it deserts, grasslands, or ice fields; it could land safely anywhere. But the main ship "Spark" lacked this capability. The design goal of the "Spark" main ship was efficient interstellar transit, focusing on long-distance flight between Earth and Mars orbits. According to the original plan, after completing the Mars mission, "Spark" would eventually return to a low Earth orbit. Then, specialized space barges would launch from Earth, rendezvous with "Spark" in low orbit, and safely bring the astronauts back to the ground. Meanwhile, the barges would also replenish "Spark" with new propellant, consumables needed for the life support systems, and supplies for subsequent missions, bringing the astronauts for "Pillar of Heaven VI" or "Pillar of Heaven VII." This way, "Spark" would be able to depart again for its next interstellar exploration mission.

If we relied entirely on "Spark's" ion propulsion system for sustained reverse thrust, using only orbital corrections to send us back to Earth, it would take at least three days. By then, the situation would only become more out of control, giving rise to many complications.

After an urgent and intense discussion, we finally formulated a bold and extremely risky plan: First, discard the cargo bay. Then, simultaneously activate the engines of both the main ship "Spark" and the "Stardust" landing craft. Although the combined thrust of both was insufficient to complete a major maneuver, it was enough for a critical course correction—deflecting the spacecraft toward the moon, utilizing the moon's gravity to decelerate and make a sharp turn, slingshotting us back to Earth.

The ensuing problem was that we could not land together.

"Spark" did not have full atmospheric reentry capabilities. It had neither a chemical main engine, nor parachutes, nor landing gear. If it had to return to Earth, it could only undergo a long and dangerous ion reverse thrust deceleration outside the atmosphere, relying on multiple "skip braking" maneuvers grazing the edge of the atmosphere, ultimately handing over the braking to the Qixi Space Center's maglev capture track.

Therefore, upon entering low Earth orbit, "Stardust" would separate from "Spark." "Stardust" would directly enter the atmosphere, utilizing friction to decelerate. While "Spark" would need to sustain deceleration outside the atmosphere, slowing to Mach 8 before entering the atmosphere and gliding to the space center.



Xiao Ma looked up after frantic calculations, sweating profusely: "Thanks to that damn nuclear fusion engine on Spark, our initial speed is extremely high now; the spacecraft will graze the moon in just two hours. We only have one chance! Using the remaining fuel in the landing craft will be enough for us to execute a max deceleration on the far side of the moon, using the lunar gravity slingshot effect to throw us back into Earth orbit! But the risk is still huge; if the angle is wrong, we could be thrown straight out of the Earth-Moon system, wandering in space forever."

I trusted Xiao Ma's calculations and said, "It's better than sitting ducks! We have no time to hesitate anymore; let's act now! We... must go back alive!"

Captain Chen and Old Zhao also approved our plan, and everyone quickly returned to their stations. Like an enraged bull, the spacecraft discarded the cargo bay, then turned its nose around and charged toward the moon.


"Five minutes to perilune!"

As the spacecraft approached, the moon outside the window rapidly expanded from a plate into an oppressively massive gray wall. Craters were clearly visible, like huge scars on the lunar surface, glaring fiercely at us.

"Prepare to enter the dark side shadow zone of the moon!"

The moment the spacecraft circled to the far side of the moon, the glow of Earth was completely blocked out. The outside plunged into profound darkness, as if even starlight had been swallowed. The massive gravity grabbed the spacecraft like an invisible giant hand, causing the metal skeleton to emit teeth-gritting creaks.

"Now! Engines full reverse thrust!"

Boom—!

The chemical rocket on the landing craft, originally intended only for short-distance ferrying, let out a hysterical roar. Through the window, I saw the massive silver body of Spark emitting horrifying sounds of metal twisting under our push. The faint blue light of the ion engine was utterly insignificant in the face of the fury of chemical flames. We were using the strength of an ant to forcibly change the running direction of an elephant.

Under the dual action of the moon's gravity and engine thrust, the spacecraft carved a terrifyingly sharp acute angle in space. We skimmed closely across the lunar surface; at our closest, it felt as though we could reach out and touch those icy ridges.

"Exiting shadow zone! Course correction complete! Target: Earth!"

When that azure planet reappeared in our field of view, a cheer erupted in the cabin. We had succeeded; this beautiful return thrust had thrown us from a dead end in deep space back into the embrace of our homeland.

What followed was a long journey home. Along the way, we felt things growing increasingly bizarre. Not only had we lost contact with the space center, but even the civilian broadcast TV satellites had all lost their signals. Something must have happened on Earth, otherwise it wouldn't be this quiet.



Finally, the moment of separation arrived.

"Stardust, prepare for separation!" Chen Weimin's voice rang over the intercom. "Comrades, see you on the ground!"

With a crisp "click" of mechanical unlocking, Stardust slowly detached from the mothership. Through the window, I watched the massive Spark gradually drift away. Its silver-white silhouette looked so lonely yet resolute in space. It would face a longer deceleration process alone, while we would act as the vanguard, plunging straight into the atmosphere.

The landing craft's main engine fired up again, making the final attitude adjustments before landing. "Fuel consumption is five percent faster than we expected!" Xiao Ma suddenly whispered, her voice carrying a trace of tension and unease. "We need to reduce thruster power immediately, or we might not make it to the designated landing site!"

"Adjust attitude immediately, increase the angle of attack, then shut down the main engines, and try to use the air resistance of Earth's atmosphere to decelerate! We might arrive a bit earlier than scheduled, but it doesn't matter," Old Zhao commanded swiftly and calmly. "Little Ruan, you're responsible for closely monitoring cabin pressure and temperature, ensuring the life support system runs normally; there absolutely cannot be any problems!"

I quickly checked the data, confirming that cabin pressure and temperature were within safe normal ranges. The Earth outside the window grew clearer, the blue oceans and white clouds intertwining to form a magnificent and beautiful picture, breathtakingly beautiful.

"Everyone," Old Zhao reminded, "check your spacesuits, life support systems, and seatbelts again, get ready for atmospheric impact!"


"Reentry speed 11 km/s, approaching escape velocity!" Xiao Ma stared intensely at the instrument panel, her face ashen. "Stardust's heat shield was designed for Mars' thin atmosphere. Whether it can withstand the friction of Earth's atmosphere, only God knows."

"If it can't hold, it still has to!" I roared, gripping the handrails tightly. "Bring it on!"

Boom!

Without transition, without prelude, the landing craft plunged headlong into the atmosphere.

In that instant, outside the window turned into an inferno.

Scorching plasma instantly engulfed the hull, turning the pitch-black space into a blinding orange-red. The temperature rose sharply, the bulkheads grew hot, and the smell of burning filled the air.

Violent shaking made my teeth chatter, and my vision blurred. My ears were filled with the shrieking sound of air being torn apart, as if countless evil spirits were scratching at the outside of the cabin.

"Heat flux density exceeding warning level by 120%!" Xiao Ma roared her report, her voice tinged with tears. "The heat shield is ablating! Left wing surface temperature anomalous!"

"Hold the attitude! Don't move!" Old Zhao's voice was an anchor. "As long as we don't tumble, we won't burn to death!"

Like a shooting star, trailing a long tail of flames, we charged furiously above the stratosphere.


I don't know how long passed, maybe a century, maybe just a few minutes.


"Blackout zone ended, communications restored!" Xiao Ma collapsed into her chair as if drained. "Fuel remaining 7%, enough for a precision landing!"

Hearing my companion's voice again, I knew we had survived the most dangerous phase. The violent shaking and turbulence gradually weakened, and the firelight outside faded, replaced by darkness. The landing craft was rushing into the stratosphere at the speed of sound. Xiao Ma began carefully controlling the landing craft to extend a pair of cute triangular gliders, further decelerating the craft.

However, before we could even catch our breath, a harsh sound of static suddenly came over the communication channel.

"Bzz... Bzz... This is Ground Command Center."

That voice was cold, mechanical, devoid of any emotional color, making one's scalp tingle. "Calling Pillar of Heaven V, please respond if you receive this."

Old Zhao immediately opened the channel: "This is Stardust, we have encountered a severe mechanical failure, requesting emergency landing! Repeat, requesting emergency landing!"

Logically, facing the sudden return of a lost spacecraft, the ground should be in a state of chaos and joyous surprise. But at this moment, it was eerily quiet over there, as if they had long anticipated our return.

A few seconds later, that cold voice sounded again, its tone as flat as if reading a death sentence:

"Received. Cleared to land. Please follow the designated flight path and land in Zone 3."


## Phosphorescence

When the familiar control tower of the Qixi Space Center rapidly magnified in our field of view, I naively thought that as long as our feet touched the ground, we would be safe.

However, right then, Xiao Ma suddenly cried out: "Captain! Quick! Look at the thermal imaging scanner! The situation on the ground... something is very wrong on the ground!" Through the images transmitted by the thermal scanner, we discovered that the situation on the ground was far more complex and dangerous than we imagined. The runway, which should have been empty, was now surrounded by a large number of fully armed special forces soldiers. Their figures were exceedingly clear in the thermal imaging, like a pack of wolves poised to strike, waiting in tight formation for their prey.

"They... they're here to silence us!" Old Zhao's face became unusually grim. His voice was low and icy, as if he had anticipated this scene all along. There was no trace of panic or fear in his eyes, but rather a resolute determination in the face of death. "It seems they don't plan on letting us go. Everyone, listen to my orders! Activate the spacesuits' shield systems immediately! Xiao Ma, do your best to land the craft in an open area on the edge of the launch pad, away from crowds and buildings! Remember, we must stick together and hold out until Captain Chen and our brothers from Group A return and rendezvous with us!"

We quickly sprang into action. Following Old Zhao's instructions, we activated the spacesuits' shield systems. These suits, originally designed for high-intensity operations in space, now became our final line of defense. Accompanied by a low hum, a layer of eerie blue electric field energy shields lit up around us.

The retrorockets kicked up a massive cloud of yellow sand from the ground. The landing craft slammed heavily into the wasteland at the edge of the launch site like a meteorite.

The hatch had barely popped open when several fully armed special forces soldiers charged forward, surrounding the landing craft tightly. However, they clearly underestimated the power of these space engineering suits, which weighed a hundred kilograms each.

Old Zhao met two soldiers head-on; he didn't even make any extra movements. With a casual wave of his hand, the thick hydraulic arms of his exoskeleton hissed under the strain, then erupted with terrifying torque, throwing one soldier, along with his rifle, over a dozen meters away like a ragdoll. Seeing red, the servo motors in my leg exoskeleton whined furiously, assisting me in leaping several meters into the air, delivering a flying kick that smashed an enemy preparing to fire a rocket launcher, crushing both his sternum and Kevlar vest.

"Grab a vehicle! Hurry! Commandeer a vehicle for cover!" Old Zhao bellowed.

But the highly trained special forces reacted extremely fast; they immediately pulled back and deployed heavy weapons. Several armored vehicles unleashed a fierce barrage at us using machine guns and vehicular cannons, a stream of tracer rounds pouring down like rain.

"Bang bang bang—"
The dense hail of bullets struck the energy shields, sending ripples of blinding blue light accompanied by an ear-piercing high-frequency hum. Although the bullets couldn't directly penetrate the shield, the massive kinetic energy transferred to the spacesuit, rattling my internal organs. On the helmet's HUD, the progress bar representing the shield's integrity was flashing frantically.

"Warning: Shield energy continuously decreasing, currently at 72%... 65%..." The system's synthetic voice urgently announced in my ear.

"They're starting to use armor-piercing incendiary rounds! The shields won't hold!" Xiao Ma's voice over the comms was filled with obvious anxiety; the exterior of her spacesuit had already been scorched black by explosive blasts.

We were forced to retreat behind the landing craft; its massive titanium alloy landing gear became our only cover. The air reeked of pungent cordite and ozone. Huddled behind the cover, listening to the heavy mechanical gasps from the exoskeleton's joints, and watching the red warning boxes flashing relentlessly at the edge of my vision. That fear of helplessly watching our protection being stripped away, waiting for death to arrive, was even colder than space.

"Old Zhao... Captain, we can't hold out much longer..." I lowered my voice, speaking anxiously to Old Zhao through the suit's intercom. "Should we... should we just surrender? Maybe there's still a way out..."

"Naive!" Old Zhao sneered. "The underlying premise of the 'Moth' project is dead people. To them, the only good heroes are dead ones. Surrender? That's just saving them bullets!"

Just as we were running out of ammunition and food, a furious roar of engines suddenly came from the west side of the battlefield.

Two ruggedly shaped troop transports sped in from the west, their wheels kicking up clouds of dust, leaving two long trails of smoke across the empty launch site. These transports were almost identical in model to the combat vehicles used by the special forces surrounding us, but their bodies had been hastily spray-painted with large, eye-catching patches of bright red, looking exceptionally jarring against the gray background. Moreover, a few high-power loudspeakers had been roughly mounted on their roofs. The voice booming from the speakers was startlingly clear over the chaotic battlefield, instantly drowning out the sounds of gunfire:

"We are the Red Mountain Free Army! Astronaut brothers of the Space Center, do not be afraid! We are here to rescue you! We are here to save you!"

Why did this voice sound so much like Guofeng?

Guofeng? Free Army? These two words put together made my brain crash instantly. My former roommate who disappeared after failing the selection had actually become a leader of the legendary anti-government armed force that exclusively fought the government? Due to the information blockade, my previous knowledge of the Red Mountain Free Army was very limited; I thought they were just a small group of terrorists operating in remote mountainous areas, likely backed by the Western Empire. I never imagined they actually had the strength to attack our space center. Wouldn't their appearance confirm our treason?

The special forces clearly hadn't anticipated this sudden turn of events, and their formation fell into chaos for a moment. Some turned their weapons, redirecting fire toward the suddenly appearing transports; others continued shooting in our direction. The Free Army showed no weakness either; dense tongues of fire spat from the firing ports on their transports, engaging in a fierce firefight with the special forces. In the lulls between fighting, they didn't forget to shout at the government troops. The loudspeakers blared again, the voice even louder and clearer:

"Brother soldiers! Stop risking your lives for a corrupt, reactionary government! To serve their own selfish interests, they didn't hesitate to sacrifice the lives of astronauts! We have already obtained ironclad evidence of their conspiracy and made it public! Right now, the whole world is watching this place! Reporters are on their way, and the doom of the reactionary government is at hand!"

The situation became even more chaotic. Armor-piercing rounds continuously struck our energy shields, bursting with blinding sparks, and the air was thick with gunpowder and a burnt smell that made it hard to breathe. Through the sparks and smoke, I saw several transports chasing and firing at each other across the battlefield; the scene was unusually chaotic and intense, having completely devolved into a massive melee.

Just then, a dazzling light appeared in the sky above Xuanlong Ridge, racing toward us. The light grew clearer, and I shouted excitedly, "It's 'Spark'!" Captain Chen and the Group A members seemed completely unaware that the ground had turned into a chaotic battlefield; they were still following the original plan, flying toward the entrance of the maglev track.

"Oh no! They don't know what's happening on the ground! The second half of the track is destroyed! If they land like this, it'll be a disaster!" Old Zhao's voice rang out anxiously over the comm channel, filled with worry and fear.

We tried to contact 'Spark' through our suits' comm systems to warn them about the ground situation, but we heard absolutely no response from 'Spark'. 'Spark' slid into the maglev track at the top of Xuanlong Ridge, but power to the track had long been cut. Without a means to brake, Spark was like a derailed high-speed train, carrying apocalyptic kinetic energy as it barreled off the end of the track at nearly 300 km/h.

"Get out of the way!!!"

I only had time to hear Old Zhao's roar before my body instinctively rolled into a drainage ditch.

"BOOM————!"

An earth-shattering explosion. The ground heaved violently like waves, and a massive shockwave mixed with debris and metal shrapnel swept right over my scalp. Spark's colossal hull grazed the edge of the control tower, acting like a sharp plow that leveled half the launch pad, finally smashing hard into the high wall at the edge of the base. The sturdy concrete wall collapsed instantly, kicking up a cloud of dust that blotted out the sun.

The collapse of the wall sparked even greater chaos. Long before this, a large group of reporters drawn by the news had gathered outside the gates of the Qixi Space Center, trying to enter the base for interviews, but were strictly blocked by heavily armed soldiers. Now, with the wall collapsing, a massive gap appeared, and the reporters, no longer caring about the soldiers' blockades or the hail of bullets inside, flooded in like a tidal wave. Ignoring any no-fly zones, they launched hundreds of camera-equipped drones that swarmed the sky over the battlefield like locusts. With red recording lights flashing, these drones broadcasted this purgatory-like scene—the burning spacecraft wreckage, the exchanging armies, and us ragged survivors—live to every corner of the world in real-time.

"Look! That's the crashed Spark!" "Oh my god, is that a gunfight? Who are the government troops shooting at?" "Those are... the missing astronauts!"

The eyes of the entire world were instantly focused here.

The special forces soldiers were clearly panicked. Under the gaze of billions of eyes, they momentarily didn't know what to do. The gunfire gradually sparse out, and the battlefield fell into an eerie, hair-trigger standoff.


In this chaotic and noisy battlefield, I tightly clutched my helmet, looking at the distant, black-smoke-belching wreckage of Spark.

Suddenly, Old Zhao's voice sounded over the comms again, carrying an indescribable shock and fear: "Look... look toward the control tower! You guys, look over at the control tower! What... what happened over there?!" Following the direction he was pointing, I struggled to look up, and through the pervasive smoke and dust, I saw a sight that left everyone dumbfounded and utterly shocked...

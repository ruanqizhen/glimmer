# Fishing

   *1998* 

After I was discharged from the army at the end of the 1980s, I followed the trend and went to Guangdong to make a living for a few years. I saved some hard-earned money and returned to my hometown in the early 1990s, where I was elected as the village head by my fellow villagers.

At that time, in order to get the village-run plastic products factory up and running, I had to accompany guests from the county to fish by the fishpond almost every day. When people were around, I always had a face full of smiles, offering Chunghwa cigarettes one after another, and serving them with good tea and water in a well-thought-out manner, looking exceptionally attentive. But as soon as the guests left, I couldn't help but curse when I got back to the house and closed the door. Being a village head was much more complicated than being in the army. If any "immortal" (bureaucrat) wasn't worshipped adequately, there was no way to get the official seal for the factory. My biggest headache was Section Chief Qian from the county industrial bureau, who was the most stringent and refused to budge. I really racked my brains over this matter.

That afternoon in 1995, driving my chugging walking tractor, I pulled a large fishing net and called a few villagers to toss about the village's east fishpond for most of the day. We chased, drove, and netted, driving all the fish into the neighboring fishpond. Before leaving, I repeatedly urged Zhou Qiang, the person in charge of the fishpond, "Tomorrow, Section Chief Qian from the county industrial bureau is bringing people to fish. You lock the fence by the fishpond well, and whatever you do, don't let the fish swim over."

The next day after nine o'clock, three black Santana cars rolled in a trail of yellow dust and majestically entered the east fishpond of the village. I intentionally feigned illness that day and didn't accompany them personally. Later, Zhou Qiang told me that you could tell some of them were already regulars. As soon as they got out of the car, they led the others, lined up along the fishpond, propped up their parasols, opened their bait boxes, put on earthworms and wine-soaked rice, cast their fiberglass fishing rods, and quickly got into the zone.

But how could there still be any fish in the fishpond? After the time it takes to eat a meal, the floats on the water remained perfectly still. A few impatient guys couldn't hold back anymore. Looking around, they spotted the old fishpond next to it. After hesitating for a while, they shouldered their fishing rods and moved over. Before long, the other seven were also attracted by the screams and splashing sounds from over there, and they all followed suit. A burst of cheerful chatter and laughter immediately erupted by that fishpond.

Just as the anglers were fishing with wild abandon, an old man in his sixties appeared by the fishpond at some point. He stood with his hands on his hips, his callused face flushed red, and cursed loudly, "Alright, you bunch of robbers! I was wondering why the fish in my pond lost money year after year. It turns out that people dare to rob in broad daylight! Stand up, all of you!"

The crowd quickly stood up, looking at each other in dismay. Among them, the slightly chubby Section Chief Qian put on an apologetic smile and said, "Old uncle, we thought this was the village's public pond. We are sorry, we will pay market price for the fish we caught."

The old man was not someone easy to talk to. He stomped his walking stick on the ground, "Pay only when you're caught? When my fish were alive and kicking in the water, they were worth a big price. If you want to leave, leave all your cars behind."

Section Chief Qian got a little anxious and said, "Old uncle, please calm down. We were invited by Gao Dayong, the village head of Shuanghe Village."

The old man's anger grew even bigger, his spit flying, "The village head is a piece of shit. Let me tell you, I've lived to this age and I'm not afraid of heaven or earth. I specialize in dealing with you officials."

Seeing that the time was right, Zhou Qiang immediately rode his motorcycle to my house and brought me to the fishpond. As soon as Section Chief Qian saw me, it was like finding a savior. He pulled me aside and whispered a few words. I then pulled the old man aside and explained the situation in a low voice. Unexpectedly, upon hearing this, the old man jumped up again and shouted loudly, "So you officials cover up for each other! I've memorized your license plate numbers, and I'm going to sue you to the central government!"

Section Chief Qian, wiping his cold sweat, quietly asked what this old man's background was. I put on a helpless face and lowered my voice, saying, "Section Chief Qian, to tell you the truth, this old guy is called Guo Benwang. He is the most difficult professional petitioner in our village. My predecessor was stubbornly sued out of office by him."

The muscles on Section Chief Qian's face twitched slightly.

I gritted my teeth again and said, "This old thing is going too far. Section Chief Qian, don't worry. I'll risk losing my job as the village head, bring two young men to tie him back, and I will absolutely not let him delay the futures of all you leaders."

Section Chief Qian waved his hands repeatedly, "Dayong, please don't meet force with force. For this kind of grassroots masses, we must do careful ideological work and cannot intensify conflicts. I leave this matter entirely in your hands. You must appease him well. As for the difficulties of your village-run factory, the county will help solve them as soon as possible."

Later, all the approval procedures for setting up the plastic products factory indeed got the green light and were all approved.

A few evenings later, I invited the old man to the Juxian Teahouse in town, respectfully poured him a cup of hot tea, and said, "Second Uncle, I really owe it all to you this time!"

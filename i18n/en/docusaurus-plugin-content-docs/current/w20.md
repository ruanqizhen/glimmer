# Your Answer

*2007*

## The Calm Before the Storm

Just past seven o'clock on a Friday morning, the city was enveloped in a hazy mist. I wrapped my jacket tightly around myself and walked briskly on my way to work. Logically, it should have been getting warmer by early March, but the temperature this year was extremely abnormal. A gust of cold wind hit my face, and the damp, cold air drilled into my neck through my collar, making me shiver uncontrollably. I subconsciously pulled my collar up, complaining inwardly, "What's with this damn weather? Why is it still like winter?"

As the weekend approached, the pressure at work hadn't eased at all. Our project was at a critical stage right before delivery and acceptance, and the client kept asking for changes here and there, leaving the entire development team overwhelmed. I had worked overtime until eleven last night before dragging my exhausted body home, and I had to rush back to the office early this morning to deal with the flood of demands. As I walked, I quickly went through the list of problems I needed to handle in my mind, hoping to sort out my thoughts as soon as possible.

Right then, the cell phone in my pocket suddenly rang, its crisp ringtone sounding particularly abrupt on the quiet path. I stopped, took out my phone, and saw that the area code was from the United States.

A long-distance call from the US? It should be Xiaopeng. He flew to Boston last week on a business trip to communicate with the client about product delivery. It should be around 6 PM in Boston now, so he probably just got off work. Calling me at this time—could there be another emergency change in the project?

Thinking of this, a trace of unease rose faintly in my heart. I had already entered the software park, should I wait until I get to the office to return the call? Forget it, I picked up the phone anyway: "Hello, is this Xiaopeng?"

"Qizhen! Something terrible has happened!" Xiaopeng's hurried voice came from the other end of the line. The background was mixed with noisy voices, piercing sirens, and the faint sound of broadcasts in the distance, as if he were in the middle of a chaotic scene. "Listen to me!" His voice carried an obvious panic, even sounding somewhat incoherent.

Hearing Xiaopeng's anxious tone, my unease deepened, and I quickly asked, "What's wrong? What are they dissatisfied with now?"

Xiaopeng hastily interrupted me: "Stop talking about the project! Let me tell you, CBS News over here is urgently broadcasting a disaster warning, saying that there's unprecedented abnormal activity in the atmosphere. Experts say that a global disaster might happen in the next few days, and they don't even know the specifics!"

The sirens in the background were getting closer and louder, as if a large number of vehicles were being urgently evacuated. I could even hear the screams and cries of a crowd in the distance.

"It's a complete mess over here. The streets are full of people panic-buying like crazy. I just got back from the supermarket, and the shelves are completely empty, they're even out of toilet paper. The news must be blocked back in China, you need to go hoard some food and water quickly, hoard as much as you can! Stop dawdling!" He spoke extremely fast, his voice laced with panic, even trembling slightly.

I looked up blankly, scanning my surroundings. The street was still calm, with only a few pedestrians walking by hurriedly, completely unaware of any so-called disaster. The tall buildings in the distance loomed in the morning mist, and the sky was still gray—aside from being slightly gloomier than usual, there were no other obvious abnormalities.

"Are you serious? A global disaster? Have you been watching too many Hollywood blockbusters lately?" I was a little spooked by his words and tried to confirm: "Communications are so advanced now, if something this huge really happened, the internet would have exploded a long time ago, right?"

"Damn it! Do you think I'm joking with you?!" Xiaopeng almost roared, his voice full of despair. "Look up at the sky yourself, doesn't it look a bit off? The sky over here has turned green, and the clouds are in rings. This is absolutely not a normal phenomenon!"

I looked up at the sky. The sky did seem to be gloomier than when I left the house this morning, and dark clouds were gathering in the distance.

"Also, my wife has been sick these past few days and has been staying at home. Please remember to bring her some food and water, I'm begging you, brother!" Xiaopeng's voice was pleading, even choking up a bit.

"Of course, we're brothers, your wife is my wife!" I replied in my usual joking tone, trying to cover up my own panic.

"Help me out, please! I'm being serious with you, don't take this as a joke!" Xiaopeng's tone suddenly became unusually stern, making my heart skip a beat.

"Okay, I got it, I'm going to the supermarket right now." I realized I went a bit too far with the joke, dropped the humor, and answered seriously: "You take care of yourself too."

Suddenly, an ear-piercing howl of electric microphone feedback came from the other end of the line, and then it abruptly cut off. I looked at the dark screen, and news stories I had seen over the years flashed through my mind: environmental destruction, global warming, melting glaciers... my heart sank heavily. Xiaopeng was right; this kind of thing was bound to happen sooner or later.

I rushed into the company building, taking the stairs two at a time. The lobby was empty, except for the duty security guard yawning at the surveillance screens. I didn't even bother to greet him; I just swiped my card, got into the elevator, and mashed the "close door" button.

The elevator stopped on the eighth floor. I walked into the office area and spotted "Brother Monkey" at a glance. She was wearing a bright red plaid shirt today, which stood out in the drab office. It was good that she was here.

Hou Yanan joined the company two years earlier than I did and was the only female colleague in our department. However, after hanging out with a bunch of us science guys for so long, she had also become carefree and straightforward, a total "tomboy". She recently bought a new model of a "Baolv X5" SUV, becoming the first programmer in our department to own a private car. In her spare time, she loved to show off the various features of her car, from the smart headlights to the panoramic sunroof, wishing the whole company knew she had bought a new car. Xiaopeng's home was a bit far from the company, and if I needed to buy a lot of supplies, her car would come in handy.

Brother Monkey was leaning back in her ergonomic chair, holding a steaming cup of milk tea to warm her hands while frowning at a string of red network connection errors popping up on her computer screen. I leaned over and called out flatteringly, "Sister Hou!"

She lazily turned her chair around, the corners of her mouth curling up to reveal two large buck teeth, and said with a smile, "Oh, why are you being so sweet today? Being overly attentive for no reason, have you got your eyes on my piece of junk car and want to hitch a ride?"

I didn't have the time to banter with her and got straight to the point: "There might be a natural disaster in the next two days! Hurry up and take me to the supermarket, we need to hoard some supplies!"

"What the hell?" Brother Monkey looked at me in disbelief. "Are you listening to Xiaopeng's nonsense? When I opened my mailbox this morning, it was full of emails from that psycho, talking about the end of the world, the earth is doomed, making it sound so real! You actually believe that crap?"

I grabbed the back of her swaying chair, leaned in close, and said, "It's real this time! I just talked to Xiaopeng on the phone, heard it with my own ears, it's already a complete mess over there! If you keep dawdling here, we might not even be able to get into the supermarket later!" My tone was hurried and serious, completely lacking my usual playfulness.

Brother Monkey was stunned; I had never been this serious even when debugging code. She put down her milk tea and carefully studied my expression, trying to find a trace of a joke in my eyes. However, my face was pale, and my eyes were filled with anxiety and unease, which made her realize that things might really be bad. She frowned, and her tone became serious too: "Really? What exactly happened?"

I let go of her chair, pointed to the increasingly gloomy sky outside the window, and said anxiously, "Look outside for yourself!"

Brother Monkey walked to the window, pulled the blinds all the way up, and looked out. The sky, which had only been somewhat gloomy, was now covered in dark clouds, rolling up and down like ink dropping into water. The tall buildings in the distance seemed to be swaying in the fierce wind, and the pedestrians on the street were all quickening their pace. Brother Monkey frowned and muttered, "This... this looks like a heavy downpour is coming! If we go out, I'll have to wash the car tomorrow."

"Are you going or not?!" I stomped my foot anxiously, wishing I could just drag her out.

"It's working hours right now! If the manager catches us wandering around outside, won't our bonus for this month go down the drain?" Brother Monkey was still somewhat hesitant, glancing at the elevator from time to time, as if the manager could come out of it at any moment.

"What time is it now, and you're still thinking about the bonus?" I said in exasperation. "Besides, our R&D department has flexible working hours. Isn't it normal to come in a bit late in the morning after staying up late writing code?" I tried to use the company's rules to persuade her, but she obviously wasn't buying it.

"Do you think I'm a country bumpkin?" Brother Monkey rolled her eyes, unceremoniously exposing me. "With a manager like ours, as stingy as a Scrooge, he only ever takes advantage, when does he ever take a loss?" As she spoke, Brother Monkey sat back in front of her computer screen and skillfully typed in the URL of the "Haijiao Forum", only to see the same "network connection timeout" error.

I knew that arguing with her any further would only waste time, so I used my ultimate trump card: "When you bought your Baolv, you borrowed 50,000 yuan from me, right? Either pay me back now, or drive me to the supermarket immediately!"

Brother Monkey's expression changed instantly, as if someone had stepped on her tail. She glared at me, gritted her teeth, and said, "Look how petty you are, getting all worked up." Then she turned around, grabbed her coat hanging on the back of the chair, and picked up the car keys on the desk: "I didn't say I wasn't going. Let's go, let's go, let's go!"

## Wind Rising in the Green Duckweed

In the elevator, Brother Monkey grumbled the whole way: "If this month's bonus is gone, you have to take full responsibility! I'll just deduct it from the money I owe you." I was in no mood to pay attention to her complaints, my mind entirely focused on getting to the supermarket as soon as possible to hoard supplies.

With a "ding," the elevator doors opened. Brother Monkey's black Baolv X5 was parked directly opposite the elevator doors, in the most prominent spot in the parking lot. She took out her key, pressed the unlock button, and the car's headlights flashed twice, emitting a crisp electronic "beep".

"Hurry up and get in!" Brother Monkey pulled open the door and plunked herself into the driver's seat. I quickly slipped into the passenger seat and fastened my seatbelt. She inserted the key and turned the ignition switch. At that moment, the engine let out a low roar, echoing through the empty parking lot, as if a wild beast waiting to strike was about to break out of its cage. Sitting in my seat, I also felt a vibration from the car body like a tense muscle. Then the Baolv slowly bumped out of the parking space. I frowned; I had never really understood this model of car before, but it seemed it used all of its fuel consumption to generate noise.

As we merged onto the outer ring highway, the wind grew stronger, rocking the car back and forth. The sky abruptly darkened, turning leaden gray, and an apocalyptic sense of oppression enveloped us. The music on the car radio suddenly cut out, replaced by a harsh burst of static. The crackling and hissing sounds were maddening. I reached out and twisted the tuning knob, trying to find some useful information, but no matter which channel I tuned to, there was only intermittent static, occasionally mixed with a few indistinct broadcasts, sounding like murmurs from a distant place, or even another world.

"What's going on with this radio?" I twisted the knob back and forth again, but I still couldn't get rid of the annoying static.

Brother Monkey glanced out the window: "Probably electromagnetic interference, this crappy weather changes on a dime."

Just then, the sky to our right suddenly seemed as if it had been slashed open by a giant, sharp blade. A mass of ink-black clouds began to spin frantically, as if stirred by an invisible giant hand, forming a massive vortex hundreds of meters in diameter. The center of the vortex gradually extended downwards, drawing out a thick, black column of wind, like a giant snake crawling out of the depths of hell, baring its fangs and claws as it plunged straight towards the ground. The wind column let out a low, terrifying roar, as if the entire world were trembling in its fury. Wherever the wind column swept past, trees, cars, and houses were all sucked into it without exception, torn to shreds, and then scattered everywhere as debris.

"Brother Monkey, look!" My voice trembled with extreme shock and excitement as I pointed at the terrifying scene on the right. "It's a tornado! It really is a tornado! I've only ever seen them on TV, I never expected to see one with my own eyes today, this is too shocking! If only we could get a bit closer."

Brother Monkey only took a quick glance before locking her eyes back on the road ahead. Her face turned deathly pale, fine beads of sweat seeped out on her forehead, and her arms trembled non-stop. "Are you crazy? If that thing blows over here, we'll both be blown to dust!"

Yet I was like a man possessed, pulling out my phone, leaning against the window, and taking pictures of the tornado like crazy, while muttering: "Drive a bit slower, drive a bit slower! If I capture this on camera, we can brag about it for the rest of our lives!"

"You're really asking for death..." Brother Monkey cursed, but whether out of curiosity or infected by my slightly crazed mood, she somehow miraculously lowered the speed from 120 mph to 80, and the tornado gradually caught up from the right rear.

Right at that moment, the previously quiet sky ahead also began to churn violently, and three or four more pitch-black wind columns popped up one after another, like the tentacles of some giant creature, sweeping frantically back and forth across the highway. Each wind column carried a destructive force. Wherever they passed, vehicles were scooped up and then mercilessly tossed far away, turning the highway into a scene of utter devastation in an instant. The howling wind was accompanied by rumbling thunder and lightning, and the air was filled with a pungent smell of burning.

"Holy shit! What the fuck is that?!" Brother Monkey let out a terrified scream and began to frantically change lanes and accelerate, trying to find a path to survive in the gaps between these columns of death. The Baolv's body swayed violently in the strong wind, and the tires let out ear-piercing screeches.

"Left! Take the off-ramp to the left!" I commanded loudly, my hands tightly gripping the handle on the roof of the car.

Suddenly, a strange green light flashed in the rearview mirror. The light was dark and cold, as if projected from the depths of hell. I turned my head back, looked through the window, and saw a glowing object the size of a basketball following closely behind our car. It hovered in mid-air, emitting a faint green light, like a sinister eye staring coldly at us. The most hair-raising part was that its trajectory was exceptionally steady, completely unaffected by the strong winds around it.

Brother Monkey also noticed the anomaly in the rearview mirror. She glanced at it, her face turning even paler, her lips trembling slightly. "What... what the hell is that?"

"Ball lightning!" The term suddenly popped into my mind, and I couldn't help but shout it out. "I read about it in Science magazine, it's a super rare natural phenomenon, even harder to see than a tornado! I can't believe we ran into it!" I quickly turned the camera towards the back, afraid of missing this rare sight.

Brother Monkey obviously wasn't in the mood to listen to my popular science lesson. She slammed the gas pedal to the floor, and the speedometer needle quickly broke past 160. However, the orb of light was like a lingering ghost, always biting onto us tightly, and no matter how much we accelerated, we couldn't shake it off. Looking at the approaching orb of light, I subconsciously muttered: "Can't escape, we simply can't escape!"

Suddenly, the orb burst with an even stronger green light than before, as if a massive amount of energy had instantly been injected into it, and its volume rapidly expanded, growing from the size of a basketball to almost the same size as our car! The light it emitted became even more blinding, dyeing everything around us in a bizarre green hue. Then, it leaped up, instantly cleared our car, blocked our path not far ahead on the highway, and immediately rushed fiercely towards us like a shooting star.

Brother Monkey had completely forgotten my existence, staring blankly ahead, muttering to herself: "Ahhh! I'm fighting you to the death, old lady here! I don't believe I can't shake you off..."

With that, Brother Monkey abruptly cranked the steering wheel completely to the left, and the car instantly lost control. The moment centrifugal force threw us toward the guardrail, the massive green orb didn't dodge; instead, like the gaping maw of a predator, it slammed into us head-on.

In that instant, it was as if the world's mute button had been pressed, and an indescribably viscous sensation enveloped the entire car. I felt my body become abnormally heavy, followed immediately by extreme lightness, as if the gravitational constant had undergone a violent fluctuation at that moment. Looking through the windshield, what I saw was no longer the highway, but countless distorted rays of light. The tornado, the sky, the guardrail—everything was stretched into long, thin lines, spiraling into that endless green abyss.

My thoughts became exceptionally slow; every notion took several seconds to form. I turned to look at Brother Monkey, only to find her movements frozen in an extremely bizarre slow-motion frame—her mouth was wide open in the shape of a scream, but the sound was like a low-frequency roar stretched out countless times.

I tried to reach out and grab Brother Monkey, but my arm felt as if it were bound by some invisible force, completely unable to move. The green light before my eyes grew brighter and brighter, until finally, amidst a blinding green glare, I completely lost consciousness.
## Like a Lifetime Ago

Consciousness surfaced slowly and with difficulty, like a bubble sinking into the deep sea. There was no sound of wind, no roar of an engine, and even the burnt smell in the air had vanished without a trace. I slowly opened my eyes, and my vision shifted from blurry to clear. I found myself still sitting in Brother Monkey's Baolv SUV; it was all over. Where was I?

Our car was parked in the center of a massive, hemispherical hall. The surface of the beige walls around us was covered in a crisscrossing network of raised veins, resembling blood vessels on the inner walls of some giant creature's organs. Countless glowing objects of various sizes were embedded within, casting a cold white light. The hall was filled with metal tables that looked like operating consoles, and on top of them were displayed various mechanical and electrical devices: there were familiar everyday appliances like TVs and computers, as well as some professional instruments and meters, and even many unimaginable contraptions with bizarre shapes, whose purpose was unknown. They were dismantled and scattered about, with cables dragging on the ground like intestines.

Where is this? A scrap recycling station? I rubbed my sore eyes, trying to make out the few swaying shadows in the distance. They were three... "creatures" walking upright. The moment I saw them clearly, a chill shot straight from my tailbone to the crown of my head, and my heart skipped a beat.

They were cockroaches.

No, to be exact, they were three giant arthropods, nearly two meters tall, walking upright on their hind legs. Their appearance closely resembled the American cockroach, but their size was suffocatingly huge. As a science student, the "square-cube law" that instantly flashed through my mind told me that such large insects couldn't possibly exist: their thin legs couldn't support their bodies, and their tracheae couldn't supply enough oxygen. But the reality before my eyes shattered all theories.

With every step they took, a dull vibration came from the ground. That was definitely not a sound that could be made by a few hundred pounds of biological flesh; it was more like heavy hydraulic machinery operating. Under the light, their bright brown epidermis reflected a cold gleam—that wasn't the diffuse reflection of chitin, but the cold light of some synthetic metal. They had no rise and fall of breathing, and a puff of white mist would occasionally spray from their joint connections.

Each individual possessed two pairs of arms, upper and lower. The upper arms were thick and powerful, while the lower arms were relatively slender and flexible. The structure of their "hands" was surprisingly similar to human hands, with fingers that could differentiate flexibly and perform various precise gripping actions. One of the cockroaches was skillfully tinkering with a dismantled laptop.

Upon closer inspection, their heads were completely different from cockroaches, looking more like an enlarged locust: around a large mouth were layers of countless keratinized lips. Sometimes when they opened their mouths, they would reveal sharp teeth inside, which could also open and close, making a spine-chilling grinding sound. Their heads were almost directly "squatting" above their chests, with no obvious neck, so their heads couldn't turn flexibly. But this didn't seem to hinder their vision. They had huge hemispherical compound eyes on both sides, like two giant glass balls embedded in their heads, enough to cover a 360-degree field of view around their bodies. I guessed that these structures reflecting a faint blue light must be compound eyes; a single eyeball could absolutely not grow this large.

For some reason, I thought of the small apartment I rented. Whenever I came home late at night and turned on the kitchen light, I would see cockroaches of all sizes densely covering the floor, the stove, and the walls. When the light suddenly appeared, they would act as if frightened, accelerating instantly and scrambling into various crevices with a rustle. At this moment, how I wished I could find such a crevice to squeeze into.

Brother Monkey's head leaned weakly against the seat, a trail of crystal-clear drool hanging from the corner of her mouth. I nudged her: "Brother Monkey! Wake up!"

Brother Monkey frowned and muttered incoherently, "My big Baolv...", then subconsciously touched the steering wheel, opened her eyes just a slit to confirm she was still in the car, and then closed them again.

"Brother Monkey! Take a look." I called her softly again. Brother Monkey opened her eyes, looked at me, and then glanced forward. Suddenly, Brother Monkey's pupils dilated instantly. She pressed back against the seat, her nose twitched, and she began to draw breath deep into her belly. In that moment, I saw her lungs draw in enough air for a one-minute scream. I thought to myself, "Oh no," and reached out my arm, slapping my hand over her mouth just as she opened it. However, I was a step too late; my hand was blocked by her buck teeth, and a short, piercing "Ah!" leaked out through my fingers.

The cockroaches heard Brother Monkey's cry. The flashes in their compound eyes swiveled sharply, locking onto our position. Two of the cockroaches then turned around and walked towards us with heavy steps. A rhythmic sound of metal plates scraping against each other echoed in the empty hall, making my scalp tingle and my heart race.

One of them walked up to the car, lowered its hideous head, and examined us through the window. That massive compound eye was less than ten centimeters away from me; I could even clearly see countless hexagonal ommatidia inside fine-tuning their focus.

"Bang!"

It knocked on the car window with its massive, hydraulic-pliers-like forelimb. The entire car shook violently. Although the bulletproof glass didn't shatter, it was instantly covered in a spiderweb of cracks.

Seeing that the window didn't break, it seemed to lose patience. It reached out its thick upper arm and grabbed the door handle. With a heart-stopping sound of tearing metal, the entire car door was forcibly ripped off as if it were made of paper and casually tossed aside.

Brother Monkey and I were roughly dragged out of the cabin like two chicks waiting to be slaughtered. Its "hands" were cold and hard, and its grip strength was astonishing; I felt like my shoulders were going to be crushed.

Brother Monkey was completely paralyzed with fear, her legs went weak, and she clung to me, trembling all over.

One of the cockroaches emitted a string of strange, sparse sounds from the base of its thighs. The sound was like metal plates rubbing together, or some kind of high-frequency electronic noise, harsh and unpleasant to the ear. Seeing us standing dumbfounded, it casually picked up a metal stick and drove us out the door.

We were pushed out of the hall by the cockroaches using sticks. A wave of muggy air mixed with a stench hit us in the face. The setting sun spread across the horizon like blood, dyeing the low-hanging clouds dark red. Several eerie flashes of lightning occasionally tore through the clouds, briefly illuminating the devastated scene on the ground. Beneath our feet was a barren hillock; trees and bamboo lay scattered haphazardly as if trampled by a giant beast, some with their roots exposed to the air and the soil overturned.

"This place... looks a bit like Sheshan..." I muttered to myself, trying hard to recognize the surrounding landscape. Sheshan was the only high ground in Shanghai, and I had looked into the distance at the skyline of the magical city from there countless times. However, at this moment, all I could see was a thick layer of smoke obscuring the sky and swallowing all familiar landmarks. Deep in the smog, faint red lights flickered, like raging fires in the distance, or the dim glow emitted by some giant machinery in operation.

Blown by the evening breeze, Brother Monkey finally came to her senses. She tugged at my sleeve, her voice faint and despairing: "Where... where exactly is this godforsaken place? Are we already dead? This isn't hell, is it? Does hell have this much smog?"

I shook my head, my mind a complete blank. How could I explain it? But I knew I had to say something, even if it was nonsense, to somewhat soothe her mental state which was on the verge of collapse. "Don't be afraid... we are still alive. We... maybe wandered into some sort of time tunnel, or a wormhole. This... might be Earth two hundred million years in the future, and cockroaches... have become the masters here..." I forced a smile, trying to mask my inner fear with a joking tone, "It's all my fault for not paying attention to hygiene and raising so many cockroaches at home..."

Brother Monkey stared at me blankly. She opened her mouth as if to say something, but ultimately just closed it weakly. Tears silently streamed down her eyes; she was probably thinking of her parents and friends, because I was thinking of them too. We had already agreed to call our parents tomorrow, but where could they be now?

The cockroaches continued to escort us south, arriving at the edge of a valley. Looking down from the high ground, I gasped—the entire bottom of the valley had been transformed into a massive pig farm! Hundreds of pigs were crowded together, letting out uneasy grunts, sometimes low, sometimes high-pitched, and the air was filled with the foul stench of livestock. There were no visible fences around the valley, but the livestock honestly stayed within it. It seemed there must be invisible energy fences keeping them firmly penned in. Nearby, a few fat pigs were rooting in the ground, fighting over scattered blue granular feed.

"It's over... this time we're completely done for..." Brother Monkey's legs gave out, and she fell to her knees. "This is their pigpen... the two of us... they must be treating us as livestock too..."

My throat tightened, and I swallowed involuntarily. I didn't dare to imagine that terrible scenario, but judging from the cold light projecting from the compound eyes of the cockroaches escorting us, our situation was probably even worse than I imagined.

The cockroach escorting us let out a harsh hiss and prodded my back with a stick, urging us to keep moving.

"Don't worry..." I used all my strength to pull Brother Monkey up. Even in such a desperate situation, I still tried to use even the clumsiest logic to maintain a shred of sanity. "If we're just being kept as livestock... at least it means we won't be killed for the time being."

## Imprisoned

We stopped in front of the entrance to a cave. The entrance was a full five meters high and ten meters wide, and thick, rusty steel rebars were haphazardly welded together to form a crude iron grille. The rocks around the entrance were jagged and interlocking, looking as if they had been violently torn open by some brute force. Through the gaps in the iron grille, I could see that the interior of the cave was even more spacious. Inside, people were lying every which way; they were emaciated, like dried corpses, their faces completely devoid of color. Their eyes flickered faintly in the dim light, like the last weak glimmer of a dying person, making it unbearable to look at them.

A foul stench hit us in the face, rushing straight to the brain, causing my stomach to spasm and nearly making me vomit. It was the smell of excrement, sweat, rotting food, and despair all mixed together, making it feel as if the entire cave were slowly decaying and festering.

"Zzzt-zzzt-woo!" The lead cockroach let out a spine-chilling hiss. The sound was sharp and piercing, like metal scraping against rough slate, stabbing deep into the eardrums and causing waves of sharp pain. It waved a pair of pincer-like forelimbs, pointing to a small door on the grille. I understood; that meant we were to go inside.

Just as Brother Monkey was about to complain, the long stick in the cockroach's hand smashed fiercely against the rock next to us. Splinters of rock flew, hitting my cheek. We didn't dare hesitate any longer and crawled humiliatingly through the small door like dogs.

With a heavy "clank," the iron door slammed shut and locked behind us. The cockroaches turned and left, the friction of their joints against the ground making an agonizing scraping sound that echoed in the empty valley, gradually fading away until it finally disappeared into the dark shadows.

Hearing the commotion, the prisoners in the cave slowly gathered around. The setting sun cast their faces in a blur of heavy shadows, making them look like ghosts. The gazes that fell upon us were mostly numb and despairing, some wary and hostile, and only a very few eyes still held a faint trace of weak hope, like a candle in the wind, teetering on the verge of going out. The crowd parted, and a young man wearing glasses stepped forward, looking like the leader. His clothes were in tatters, his pale face was smeared with mud, and his glasses were dull and covered in scratches, but they still couldn't hide the exceptionally sharp eyes behind them.

"Newcomers?" His voice was hoarse, as if he hadn't spoken in a long time.

Seeing humans, the fear in my heart subsided a little, replaced by an urgent desire for information: "Where exactly is this place? Were you caught by those bugs too?"

The man with glasses calmly replied, "Yes."

"What year is it this year?" I asked eagerly, a sense of indescribable tension and unease welling up inside me.

The man with glasses answered softly, "2007."

"2007... it seems I guessed wrong..." I muttered to myself, my brain working in overdrive, trying to find a more reasonable explanation than time travel.

"2007, July 28th," the man with glasses added slowly, his tone so calm it was chilling. "Yes, it should be the 28th, I wouldn't misremember."

"July 28th..." I gasped, abruptly turning around and grabbing Brother Monkey's shoulders, practically roaring: "Brother Monkey! We traveled through time! We really time-traveled! Remember? It was March 2nd, we were sitting in your Baolv car, and then we were hit by ball lightning! And after that... whoosh, we're half a year in the future!"

Brother Monkey's mouth hung wide open. She obviously couldn't process this amount of information and froze in place as if her system had crashed.

I ignored her and turned back to the man with glasses, explaining eagerly: "You might not believe it, but we encountered a green glowing orb, and after crashing into it, we time-traveled... We really came from the past! Really!"

I thought he would be shocked, or at least show some surprise. However, his tone was as calm as if he were discussing what to have for dinner: "I believe you."

"You... you really believe it? Time tunnels? Time travel to the future?" Now it was my turn to be completely shocked, my tone full of doubt and confusion.

The man with glasses seemed to see through my thoughts. The corners of his mouth twitched slightly, revealing a bitter smile: "For two reasons. First, over the past few months, I've seen too many unimaginable things; there's nothing I can't believe anymore. Second," he paused, as if recalling something, "I was also caught and brought here on March 2nd. Everyone here was caught during that time. We... have already been in this hellhole for over four months."

"Ah?" Having seen two-meter-tall cockroaches, it was hard for me to be shocked by anything anymore, but I really wanted to understand what exactly had happened: "Do you know what happened?"

The man with glasses looked up, took a deep breath, collected his thoughts, and then slowly began to speak: "My name is Zhou Anyu, just call me Xiao Zhou. I used to work at the Institute of Physics, Chinese Academy of Sciences." He held out a grimy hand. "Welcome to hell."

"My name is Ruan Qizhen, and this is my colleague Hou Yanan. We do software development." I gave a brief introduction. "Dr. Zhou, since you study physics, do you know what happened? Are those bugs aliens?"

Zhou Anyu found a relatively clean rock to sit on and motioned for us to sit as well. He then explained: "On March 2nd, I had just finished attending an international seminar and was flying back to Shanghai from Beijing. As the plane was about to land, I looked out the window and, behind the dark clouds, I faintly saw a massive silhouette..."

He paused here, a trace of indescribable shock and fear revealing itself in his eyes: "It was an aircraft, extremely huge, unimaginably huge. The Boeing 737 I was on was like a tiny boat in front of an aircraft carrier. This was absolutely not something Earth's technology could build. I've studied for so many years, and all the knowledge I've acquired cannot explain its existence, not even touching upon its basic principles. Then, it suddenly pierced through the clouds and dove straight down towards our plane. The next second, I lost consciousness. When I woke up again, I was already here. Zhou Anyu nodded and continued: "As for the missing time you experienced... I can't give a definitive explanation. He paused, as if weighing his words.

"As for the 'ball lightning' that hit you, it obviously wasn't a normal plasma sphere either." Dr. Zhou's speaking speed gradually increased, and his eyes lit up. "According to general relativity, it might have triggered a severe distortion of spacetime curvature locally. To use an analogy, if normal spacetime is like a flat bedsheet, that orb of light was a heavy lead ball that instantly smashed a very deep crater into the bedsheet, trapping you inside."

"When that orb of light enveloped you, you were essentially trapped on the edge of an event horizon where the flow of time was nearly at a standstill." He finally gave his conclusion:

"Simply put, you fell into a bottomless 'crevice in time.' An instant in that crater was equivalent to four months outside. You were 'hidden' in the folds of spacetime for a full four months."

I tried hard to maintain a profound and thoughtful expression, nodding cooperatively from time to time and whispering, "Mmm..." This was a conditioned reflex I had developed over years of listening to managers bragging during PowerPoint presentations: my soul had gone offline long ago, but my face had to stay online.

When Dr. Zhou finally finished his grand and self-consistent theory, the air suddenly grew quiet. Logically, I should have politely asked a follow-up question to show that I understood. But I knew very well that the moment I opened my mouth, I would expose my ignorance. Just at this awkward juncture, Brother Monkey came to the rescue like a deity descending from heaven. She threw out a much more crucial and realistic question: "What did those giant bugs catch us for? It couldn't be... to eat us as meat, could it?"

In the corner, a woman huddled into a ball let out a neurotic laugh: "Eat? That would be a quick end... They don't eat humans. They just use us as batteries, as livestock, as the cheapest labor tools..."

The surrounding crowd began to talk in low voices, and oppressive fear spread like a plague; the air was thick with the scent of despair. Brother Monkey's heart, which had just relaxed, tightened again, and the hand gripping the corner of my shirt began to tremble once more.

"We are the labor force," Zhou Anyu added coldly. "Those pigs are the food. We are responsible for feeding the pigs, building houses, and transporting materials. As long as we can still work, we won't die; those who can't work anymore... are thrown out to feed the wild beasts."

"In the past three months, you are the only newcomers sent in." Zhou Anyu's voice was low and slow, as if squeezed from deep within his throat, carrying a deep sense of helplessness and sorrow. "The humans outside... I'm afraid... there are very few left."

The air froze, and despair washed over us like a tidal wave.

I took a deep breath, forced myself to calm down, and asked in the calmest tone possible: "Have you guys... ever thought about escaping?"

These words were like a forbidden spell; the crowd that had been talking in low voices instantly fell silent. Several people looked at me in horror, as if looking at a madman, then quickly scattered and retreated to the deepest part of the cave.

Outside the iron grille, the last trace of the setting sun sank into the distant bamboo forest. The sky was like a thick black cloth, completely obscuring the moon and stars. Not far away, it seemed faintly that two cockroaches were watching us. Inside the grille, waves of nauseating, fishy stench wafted from the depths of the cave, suffocating us. On the rock ceiling of the cave, dim lights flickered on and off. Those lights were not stationary, but moved slowly, squirming like some living creature crawling in the dark, casting shadows on the cave walls that twisted and deformed, like ghostly apparitions, making one's blood run cold.

Near the cave entrance, I managed to find a slightly ventilated corner and decided to settle there. Only then did I realize that I had long been soaked in sweat, and I was actually still wearing that jacket. I took off the jacket and found that it had become filthy at some point, and it was even torn in several places. Since I wouldn't need it anyway in this weather, I shook the jacket out, spread it on the ground, and curled up on it to lie down. Brother Monkey also silently leaned close, her body trembling continuously.

That night, I barely closed my eyes; Zhou Anyu's words kept swirling in my head. As I thought about it, the only comforting thing was that my parents were actually safe back in our hometown. If this really was an alien invasion, a huge city like Shanghai would definitely be a primary target. My hometown, Xinhao Village in Keshan County, only had a few dozen households; the aliens probably wouldn't even bother to glance at it. Had I known this would happen, I would have been better off staying home and farming. Brother Monkey's hometown of Jiagedaqi was also quite safe, bordering the great forest, making it easy to hide. Yes, as long as we could escape and return home, we should be safe.

Although Brother Monkey was no longer as panicked as before, her body would still twitch from time to time, seemingly indicating a sleepless night for her as well. Outside the cave, the mournful howls of wild beasts occasionally rang out, as well as a roar similar to helicopter propellers, which was exceptionally grating in the silent night sky, terrifying to the soul. It wasn't until a sliver of pale light appeared in the east that my consciousness gradually blurred, and I groggily slipped into a state half between sleep and wakefulness.
## Ant-like Existence

Suddenly, a sharp knocking sound shattered the tranquility of the early morning, like a sharp blade piercing the silent night sky. I snapped my eyes open. Outside the iron grille, there was no sign of the cockroaches, only seven or eight burly men standing there. They held black metal clubs, the smooth surfaces of which reflected a cold light in the dim morning glow, sending shivers down one's spine. With gruff voices, they shouted loudly: "Get up! Get up! All of you, get the fuck up quickly!" while fiercely striking the iron grille with their clubs. The piercing sound of metal clashing echoed through the valley, making one's eardrums ache.

These people were our overseers, the running dogs of those alien cockroaches, and the group most feared by all the prisoners. Privately, we called them "nymphs," meaning cockroach offspring. The earliest among them to become a nymph was a man named "Li Guizi", who was originally a hooligan boss roaming society. Rumor had it that not long after being thrown into the cave, he relied on his ruthless and cunning methods to quickly bring a few lackeys under his control, and then became a local tyrant inside the cave.

Once, Li Guizi accidentally discovered that a few people were secretly planning to escape, and he immediately ran to inform the cockroaches. The cockroaches seemed to understand his meaning and quickly took action, capturing all those who planned to escape. Afterwards, Li Guizi personally beat the recaptured people to death to claim credit from the cockroaches. His cruelty and loyalty won the "appreciation" of the cockroaches. As a reward, he was allowed to leave the dirty, damp cave and move into a relatively clean cabin on the high ground.

Later, Li Guizi's subordinates also moved to the high ground one after another, and even a few somewhat attractive female prisoners were forced to live with them. Since then, Li Guizi and his underlings completely devolved into the running dogs of the cockroaches, aiding the tyrants and intensifying their oppression of the other prisoners.

The nymphs not only received better food and relatively comfortable living quarters, but were even allowed to use some low-level technological equipment, such as walkie-talkies and stun batons, which became their capital to show off their power. In exchange, they had to ensure that the other humans completed the work assigned by the cockroaches on time and mercilessly suppress any signs of rebellion.

At the construction site, the nymphs were even more vicious and cruel than the cold cockroaches. They held long stun batons and paraded arrogantly through the crowd, as if possessed by fierce demons. If they were dissatisfied with anyone, they wouldn't hesitate to swing the stun batons in their hands, fiercely lashing at those unlucky prisoners. Accompanying the "crackle" of electric shocks were the agonized screams of the prisoners, echoing in the sky above the construction site, chilling one to the bone.

Just then, a tall, thin nymph walked up to Brother Monkey and me. He swept a cold glance over us, the corners of his mouth curling into a sinister smirk, a smile full of disdain and malice. He raised the stun baton in his hand and lightly tapped the switch at the bottom with his finger. With a crisp "crackle," a dazzling blue-white arc of electricity immediately burst from the top of the baton, dancing restlessly in the air and emitting a heart-palpitating, low sizzle. The electric arc flashed on his gaunt face, making his already sinister expression look even uglier, like an evil spirit crawling out of hell.

"Hey, you two, move your fucking asses faster! Don't think you can avoid this by playing dumb!" he said in a sarcastic tone, his voice full of threat and mockery. He then waved the stun baton in front of us, the dancing electric arc only centimeters from my face. I could even clearly feel a faint heat radiating from the air, along with a subtle burnt smell. I had no doubt that if this thing really landed on me, it wouldn't just be split skin and torn flesh; I would probably be cooked straight through.

Faint morning light drifted in through the cave entrance like gossamer threads, barely illuminating a corner of the cave. The prisoners, like a horde of walking corpses, walked out of the cave one after another, their footsteps dragging and heavy, as if every step drained all the strength from their bodies. Their faces were expressionless, their eyes hollow and numb, as if they had lost all hope. Brother Monkey and I silently blended into the crowd, following this silent procession to a spacious, somewhat empty hall on the north slope.

In the center of the hall was a podium, on which two cockroaches were already standing. A bloated nymph, waddling as he walked, immediately rolled up like a ball. His round face was flushed red with excitement, and using the language of the cockroaches, he chattered out a few "zzt-creak" sounds. Good heavens, this guy had actually learned the cockroach language! After the cockroaches turned and left, the translator wiped the sweat from his forehead while trotting over to Li Guizi, bending over and whispering a few words to him.

Afterward, Li Guizi struggled up onto the podium with his short, thick legs, his hands resting on his protruding belly. His bloated face was covered with an obsequious smile, exposing his yellow teeth. A knife scar stretching from the corner of his left eye all the way to his chin twisted continuously along with his smile. That smile looked even more repulsive than his distorted flab. He opened his mouth wide and let out a sharp, piercing sound, as if deliberately mimicking the cockroaches' sound of metal scraping against glass: "Fellow villagers! The Locust Army is truly benevolent to us! They have brought food so we won't starve to death! We must know how to be grateful! Serving the Locust Army is our natural blessing!"

He paused, continuously rubbing his thick, fleshy palms, his greasy gaze sweeping over the numb crowd below like a venomous snake, occasionally sticking out his scarlet tongue to lick his lips, as if waiting for agreement and response.

However, the only response he got was dead silence. The occasional despairing coughs from the audience sounded particularly clear and grating in the empty hall. Li Guizi seemed to have grown accustomed to this; he waved his hand unconcernedly, as if shooing away flies, and ordered his subordinates to start distributing "breakfast."

Everyone received a large handful of blue granular objects, emitting a faint fishy and musty smell. Wasn't this the pig feed I saw at the pig farm yesterday? Cupping this handful of "feed", my stomach churned, and a strong wave of nausea rushed up my throat.

At this moment, a burly middle-aged man squeezed next to me. His face was full of exhaustion, his eye sockets sunken, as if he hadn't closed his eyes for days and nights. He whispered, "Endure it and eat... just one meal a day, if you don't eat... you won't be able to hold on..." He swallowed hard, his Adam's apple bobbing as if he were trying to psych himself up, then grabbed a handful of "feed," forced it into his mouth, and chewed with difficulty, the muscles on his face contorting painfully as he did so.

"My name is Zhang Tieshan. Before I was caught... I was a warehouse manager at Jiajiale Supermarket..." the middle-aged man introduced himself, his voice hoarse and low, his tone revealing a weather-beaten ruggedness.

I hesitated for a moment, then grabbed a small pinch of "feed" and bit the bullet, putting it in my mouth. The stuff had no flavor at all, just a rough, abrasive texture, as hard as rubber, and every swallow felt like swallowing sand, scraping my throat raw. For the first few days, I had the urge to vomit every time, but hunger is the strongest driving force and the most ruthless teacher. Gradually, I also became numb, learning to treat these unpalatable things as the only hope to sustain my miserable existence.

The following days were like a never-ending nightmare. Brother Monkey was assigned to the pig farm, responsible for feeding those animals and cleaning up feces every day. As for me, along with the majority of the people, I was left on the north slope, the high ground above the valley. This was an open and desolate area where nothing grew. Near the hall where we ate breakfast, the skeletons of strangely shaped, unfinished buildings stood abruptly. Each building presented a bizarre geometric shape, as if they were products of some design concept we couldn't comprehend. This was our "workplace."

Without any modern mechanical equipment, we could only rely on the most primitive manual labor. Under the brutal shouting and whipping of the nymphs, we used rough bamboo strips and some of the crudest tools possible to erect one towering building skeleton after another. The buildings were built higher and higher, and in some places, we even had to climb bare-handed to heights of over twenty meters. Without any safety measures, a moment of carelessness meant falling down. Every climb was like dancing on the blade of a knife, brushing past the grim reaper.

As a programmer, my most strenuous physical activity every day used to be typing on a keyboard. Severely lacking exercise, my bodily functions were already extremely weak. Such heavy physical labor quickly exhausted me. Fortunately, Old Zhang often secretly looked after me. Not only did he share some of my workload, but he also taught me how to slack off and avoid the attention of the nymphs.

After the skeletons of the buildings were erected, we would be ordered to use spray cans to spray a viscous, slightly sweet-smelling yellow liquid onto the surface of the bamboo strips. After being sprayed, a plant similar to lichen would quickly breed on the surface of the bamboo strips. They clung tightly to the bamboo like vines, spreading rapidly and filling every gap between the bamboo strips. These plants seemed to possess extremely vigorous vitality; in just a few days, they could completely cover the exterior walls of the entire building, forming a thick and hard shell. By controlling the amount of spraying—spraying less where it grew too lush, and spraying more "nutrient fluid" where a specific shape needed to be highlighted—we shaped these bizarre buildings in this primitive and crude way.

It was the longest half month of my life. In just half a month, I witnessed a falling accident with my own eyes. That once vibrant life instantly turned into a pile of twisted remains, and was then dragged away by the nymphs like garbage. It was like a heavy hammer, fiercely striking our fragile nerves, reminding us: the grim reaper lurks in the darkness, ready to harvest the next life at any moment. It was like an endless nightmare, leaving one unable to breathe, unable to escape.

This morning, I once again climbed to the top of the scaffolding, tens of meters high. Looking around, the thick smog sealed off everything. The tall buildings of the past could no longer be seen in the line of sight, only vast forests. I pondered to myself: why didn't I notice before that Shanghai's greening rate was so absurdly high? I pulled my gaze back, lowered my head, and haphazardly tapped my hammer now and then, while my eyes began to sneak peeks at the ground. Below, those few nymphs were wandering listlessly in the open space. Just a glance told me that this bunch of guys was also just going through the motions.

After observing for about an hour, I turned my head and whispered to Old Zhang: "I found out that only twice a day, in the morning and evening, when the cockroaches come to inspect, these nymphs act like they're on steroids, jumping all over the place. The rest of the time, they slack off just like us. There are less than 10 nymphs in total, they can't possibly keep an eye on all hundred or so of us. Just now, I saw someone secretly slip into the woods with my own eyes, and the nymphs didn't even notice."

Old Zhang understood what I meant. He shook his head and warned in a low voice, "Don't even think about it. Look at the pig farm in the valley, do you see any fences? No, right? Those fences are invisible; any pig that touches them gets shocked until its skin splits and flesh tears. We have the same fences around us, but we don't know where they're hidden. If you accidentally touch them, you're done for. The people who tried to escape before were all personally executed by Li Guizi, dying a miserable death."

"I won't let them catch me," I replied resolutely.

"Sigh," Old Zhang sighed and said no more.

At night, returning to the dark and damp cave, that foul stench mixed with sweat, decay, and despair was still so strong it was suffocating, as if it would stifle a person to death. Pale "flesh-lamp bugs" hung from the rock ceiling overhead. Occasionally, those fleshy bulbs would extend some slender tentacles, crawling slowly like worms, and then suddenly drop down, casting erratic, eerie shadows on the walls that bounced around, adding to the terrifying atmosphere.

The people in the cave were scattered on the ground like a pile of broken rag dolls, some curled up into a ball, twitching continuously, some sprawled on their backs motionless, as if already dead.

I leaned silently against the cold wall. Brother Monkey sneaked over, pulling out a large handful of feed from her pocket and handing it to me; she had stolen it from the pig farm. My eyes grew a little moist. I turned my head to look at a depression nearby; it was originally the bed of the man who had fallen, and now it was just a piece of cold, empty ground.

"Brother Monkey..." I said in a low voice, my tone filled with powerlessness and despair, "I don't know how much longer I can stay in this hellhole. Maybe one day I'll go to the construction site and never come back..."

Hearing my words, the fear and despair Brother Monkey had been forcibly suppressing finally erupted like a bursting dam. She suddenly threw herself onto my shoulder, her body trembling violently, and suppressed cries and wails came out intermittently: "I can't take it anymore! This crappy place isn't meant for humans at all! Half a month... I reek all over and haven't had a bath! We don't even have toilet paper here! ...Wuwu... is this a life meant for a human? I might as well climb up to the roof tomorrow too, and then... then jump off... end it all..."

My heart tightened, and I quickly hugged Brother Monkey's trembling shoulders, wanting to comfort her, but I found myself at a loss for words. I was usually fine with cracking jokes, but I was not good at comforting girls. After holding it in for a long time, I finally squeezed out a dry sentence: "Well... you don't need to be too sad. In our cave, the walls are covered with water everywhere, you can... you can wash your hands anytime."

## Escaping from the Jaws of Death

Comforted by my words, Brother Monkey's crying grew louder. The prisoners in the cave had long since lost interest in the sorrow of others. But at this moment, we still attracted some curious glances from those around us. I was immediately at a loss for what to do. Right then, a familiar figure quietly walked over; it was Old Zhang.

"Stop crying!" Old Zhang quickly scanned our surroundings, lowered his voice, and spoke with a trace of imperceptible excitement: "We have hope! I found something, come take a look with me..."

Hearing this, Brother Monkey immediately stopped crying and haphazardly wiped the tears from the corners of her eyes. Sharing joys and sorrows over the past half month had built an unbreakable trust between us and Old Zhang. In this despairing world, he had become our most important pillar of support.

"What is it?" I asked cautiously, unable to suppress a trace of curiosity rising in my heart. Old Zhang's cautious and excited expression made me realize that what he found must be significant.

"Follow me... don't make a sound!" After Old Zhang finished speaking, he turned and walked deeper into the cave. His footsteps were as light as a cat's, making almost no sound. Brother Monkey and I exchanged a glance, then carefully followed behind him.

The depths of the cave were even more complex and profound than we had imagined. The dark passageways extended in all directions, winding and twisting like a maze, and also resembling the internal organs of some giant creature. When walking through them, there was always an illusion of being swallowed by the darkness, and an indescribable sense of fear arose spontaneously. Many branching paths were eerie and long, leading who knows where. The walls were covered with a layer of sticky, cold substance, faintly glowing with weak phosphorescence in the dark, making one's blood run cold, not knowing if it was a natural substance in the cave or the secretion of some alien creature.

After walking for about two minutes, Old Zhang stopped in front of an extremely hidden corner. The air here was even damper and colder. The walls were covered with dense cracks, like twisted faces, and occasionally, the sound of water droplets falling from the rock crevices could be heard, exceptionally clear in the silent darkness. Old Zhang pointed to an extremely narrow entrance, gesturing for us to go in. We carefully squeezed inside and found a smaller cavern, the floor littered haphazardly with broken circuit boards and machine parts.

"Why are these things here?" I squatted down, picked up a circuit board, looked it over, and put it down again.

Old Zhang shrugged triumphantly, a crafty smile appearing on his face. He lowered his voice and said mysteriously: "I just found this too. These things... were secretly collected by that kid Zhou Anyu. Hmph, do you even need to ask what he wants to do? He definitely wants to use this junk to pull off something big—most likely he wants to escape! Although I don't know exactly what his plan is, think about it, what would happen if we told the cockroaches about this? We would definitely receive special treatment! Maybe we could even get out of this hellhole because of it, and we wouldn't have to suffer like this anymore!"

Hearing this, I was instantly stunned. A wave of indescribable disappointment and anger surged into my heart, instantly diluting my previous curiosity. I looked at Old Zhang in disbelief, my voice turning icy cold: "Old Zhang, no matter what you think of Zhou Anyu, he was captured along with us; he's our companion! Doing this will kill him! If he's taken away by those cockroaches, don't you know what his fate will be? Yes, I also want to leave here, I also want to escape this dark hellhole, but at least... at least I want to walk out of here like a human being! Do you really want to go out and be a dog for those bugs just to drag out your miserable existence?" Although my voice was very low, my tone was filled with undisguised accusation and disdain.

Old Zhang choked on my words, his face turning pale and green alternately. His eyes flickered, not daring to look me in the eye, but soon, that rogue-like expression returned to his face. He snorted coldly and said disdainfully: "Hmph, taking good intentions for a donkey's liver and lungs! I'm pointing out a bright path for you! Since you don't know what's good for you, consider me meddling in other people's business! You go on, just pretend you never knew me from now on!" After saying that, he turned his head away, staring fixedly at a flesh-lamp bug struggling to crawl on the wall, ignoring us.

At this time, Brother Monkey gently tugged at the corner of my shirt and advised softly: "Forget it... let's just get out of here quickly, to save ourselves from more trouble..." Her tone was full of anxiety and worry, clearly not wanting to argue with Old Zhang any longer.

I sighed, knowing that now was not the time to argue, and took Brother Monkey's hand, ready to leave this unpleasant cavern. However, just as we walked to the door, we ran right into Zhou Anyu coming in from outside. Seeing the three of us there, his face first showed a trace of surprise, then darkened, and his eyes grew sharp. He questioned in an icy tone: "What... are you doing here?"

His gaze quickly swept over the scattered wreckage and circuit boards on the ground, his face instantly turning livid, his eyes filled with vigilance and hostility. His voice became even colder, even carrying a trace of murderous intent: "You... want to betray me?!"

Before he even finished speaking, two more people followed him in; they were his two trusted henchmen. The moment those two saw the situation in the cavern was wrong, their eyes immediately widened with anger, glaring at us fiercely, and they even clenched their fists, looking as if they were ready to attack at any moment.

I subconsciously glanced at Old Zhang standing in the corner, only to find that he was pretending to be completely uninvolved in the matter. He stood there stiffly like a block of wood, with his back to us, still pretending to stare at the flesh-lamp bug on the wall. Seeing his ugly display, the anger in my heart burned even stronger, but I also knew now was not the time to lash out. I could only forcefully suppress the anger in my heart and force a stiff smile, trying to bluff my way through: "No... nothing! The three of us couldn't sleep, so we came out for a walk... just happened to walk here, saw this place was quite strange, so we came in to take a look... What is all this messy stuff on the ground? Heh... forget it, we won't bother you, we're leaving right now! Leaving right now!"

As I spoke, I pulled Brother Monkey, trying to leave the cavern, but Zhou Anyu and his two henchmen obviously had no intention of letting us go easily. They quickly blocked the entrance, trapping us inside the cavern. The corners of Zhou Anyu's mouth curled into a cold smirk, his eyes full of scrutiny and suspicion, but his tone actually became much calmer: "Don't be in a rush to leave... since you're already here, why don't we sit down and have a good chat..."

He pulled me to sit on a protruding rock nearby, while his two henchmen stood on either side of the entrance like two door gods, blocking all our routes of retreat.

Zhou Anyu sighed, breaking the silence, and slowly began to speak: "Xiao Ruan, do you remember? The day you were first brought in, you asked me why we didn't run away... Actually..." He paused, his gaze sweeping over the three of us as if observing our reactions, before continuing: "Actually... how could we not want to run? We all want to leave this hellhole and return to our old lives... But... who dares to tell their thoughts to strangers? Those who tried to escape, do you know what their fate was? Of everyone caught, not a single one survived. ...We... we are also afraid of being betrayed..."

Zhou Anyu paused, his gaze moving back and forth between Brother Monkey and me, as if carefully observing our reactions, before continuing: "However, things are different now. After observing you for this period of time, I found... that you two are trustworthy."

What observation? I didn't quite understand the meaning behind his words and turned to look at Old Zhang in confusion. Old Zhang's eyes were somewhat evasive, clearly feeling guilty, as he waved his hands and explained: "I told him there was no need for this! I absolutely trust you guys! But Dr. Zhou insisted on me testing you out, saying... saying it was just to be safe. I really had nothing to do with this!"

This Old Zhang, he looked so loyal and honest, but now I couldn't tell which of his words were true and which were false. I faintly understood most of the situation in my heart, so I simply stopped beating around the bush and looked directly at Zhou Anyu: "How can we help?"

Zhou Anyu nodded, a trace of apology in his eyes: "About what happened earlier, that was my fault, please forgive me. Since everything is out in the open now, we are all one family." He pointed to the two people beside him, "Let me introduce you, this is Feng Jun, and this is Dong Siyuan. A few of us, including Old Zhang, have been planning an escape for months. But dealing with aliens is too difficult; no one has ever successfully escaped before. I do have some plans, but just the few of us can't pull them off alone. I heard from Old Zhang that in college, one of you studied cryptography, and the other studied electrical engineering, and later you even did R&D in a telecom company. These are exactly the skills we desperately need!"

Now that all humans have become slaves, are these skills still useful? My mind was full of doubts. Zhou Anyu seemed to see through my thoughts and immediately added: "Come, let me show you the results of my work over the past half year."

It turned out that the place we were in was not Zhou Anyu's actual residence. He led us out of this dim cavern and continued deeper inside. The ground beneath our feet became increasingly slippery, and the air held a cold dampness. From time to time, icy water droplets dripped from the roof of the cave onto our shoulders, making us shiver involuntarily. The light from the flesh-lamp bugs overhead grew sparse, and the passing forks in the path were pitch black, as if silent abysses were staring at us.

"Just how deep is this cave?" I asked casually.

Feng Jun picked up the conversation, his tone carrying a bit of resignation: "It's not that deep, even walking slowly, you'd reach the end in five minutes. But this place has too many forks; there are at least a dozen paths a person can walk down. Unfortunately, I've tried them all, and they're all dead ends."

We followed a winding passageway, bypassing several forks, and finally arrived at a hidden cavern. A heavy stone slab blocked the entrance. Feng Jun and Dong Siyuan pushed it open together with effort, and a blinding white light instantly spilled out.

## In the Same Boat

Zhou Anyu's workspace was much larger than the previous cavern, but it appeared chaotic and disorganized. Dismantled or modified electrical components were scattered across the floor, and the walls were densely wound with wires, like a giant spiderweb. In the corner sat several dilapidated boxes, filled with unrecognizable equipment parts. The rock ceiling overhead was covered in flesh-lamp bugs; gathered so densely, their light was far brighter than in the cave outside, even a bit glaring.

"Why are all these bugs gathered here?" I couldn't help but ask.

"They usually eat the microorganisms on the rock walls. ## Drawing a Circle as a Prison

## Crossing the Sea by Deception

A bright light flashed in Zhou Anyu's eyes; he had clearly also realized the importance of this discovery: "Since we have initially grasped the pattern of the control signals, can we use these signals to counterattack? For example... try to crack their encryption and then send some fake commands to the cockroaches to disrupt their actions, or... or directly jam their communication, completely paralyzing them?"

I smiled bitterly, shook my head, and spoke frankly: "Attempting to crack these signals is probably unrealistic. Although these aliens used some of our human hardware components, their technological level is clearly far above ours. I've already tried all the encryption algorithms we currently know of, and none of them can match their encryption method; it's simply worlds apart. This completely exceeds our current technical level and equipment capabilities. However... jamming these signals might have a certain possibility."

"How do we specifically jam them?" Zhou Anyu's tone carried a hint of urgency; he was desperate to find a path of hope to escape this desperate situation.

Pointing to another set of repetitively recurring waveform data on the screen, I explained in detail: "Within the signals emitted by the control center, I found some regular repetitive segments. Usually, such repetitive signals do not carry any actual informational content; they are most likely used for frequency calibration, system debugging, or synchronization operations. If we can record these repetitive signal segments and then use some equipment to simultaneously broadcast these recorded signals from multiple different locations, it is highly likely to cause severe interference with the cockroaches' reception of normal control commands, rendering them unable to act normally. Taking a step back, even if these jamming signals cannot completely block the cockroaches' normal communication, they will definitely generate immense confusion and interest in these familiar yet alien signals, and will inevitably prioritize finding the source of these signals. We can take advantage of their brief distraction to execute our escape plan."

After listening to my explanation, Zhou Anyu fell into a brief moment of deep thought, then suddenly slapped his thigh and excitedly said: "That's a brilliant idea! We can use our existing equipment and materials to build a few high-power signal generators specifically for jamming their communication signals! Xiao Ruan, we must get these made as soon as possible!"

The feeling of fighting for freedom is completely different from fighting for a meager salary; I am now more hardworking than ever. In a week's time, working day and night, we finally managed to rush out two high-power signal generators and six sets of improvised "lightning rods."

After all the equipment was ready, I found Zhou Anyu and laid out my meticulously planned escape strategy: "If you trust me, I have a plan - we divide into two groups and break out simultaneously from the west and south sides of the pig farm. The jailbreak operation will inevitably alert those cockroaches; at that time, I will activate the pre-recorded jamming signal and drive Brother Monkey's 'Baolv' straight south, doing my best to draw the cockroaches' attention to the south. The others will take the opportunity to evacuate to the west. If the cockroaches catch up to me, I will decisively abandon the car, use the terrain to hide, and then wait for an opportunity to rendezvous with everyone else." I drew the escape route on the improvised sandbox with my finger, emphasizing the importance of drawing the cockroaches' attention.

Zhou Anyu frowned, looking at me with heavy concern, his gaze behind the glasses filled with worry: "I trust you completely, this plan... is theoretically viable, but for you to do this... is simply too dangerous! The speed and flight capabilities of those cockroaches are no joke; once they catch up to you..." He hesitated, clearly afraid to imagine the consequences.

"You don't need to worry too much about me." I waved my hand in feigned relaxation, trying to reassure him, "With the Baolv's speed, they might not easily catch up to me. Compared to you guys fleeing on foot in the wild, I actually have an advantage. At least, I can pull away faster and buy you more time."

I paused, and said with a solemn tone: "There is one thing you must promise me. Xiao Hou will definitely ask to go with me when the time comes, but no matter what, you must stop Xiao Hou; absolutely do not let her go with me! Although it's her Baolv, she... after all, she is quite timid and lacks experience in handling sudden emergencies; I'm afraid she will hold things up at a critical moment."

Hearing this, Zhou Anyu nodded: "I understand, rest assured. I will tell her that the nymphs never allow women to go to the bamboo forest, and going there would only arouse their suspicion. During the operation, I will arrange for Old Zhang to stay by her side at all times to take care of her; he will definitely ensure Xiao Hou's safety."

Afterward, Zhou Anyu fell silent for a moment. He rubbed his chin with his hand, seemingly weighing the pros and cons of the plan. Finally, he slammed his hand on the table, made up his mind, and said: "We will do as you say. Tomorrow night I will gather everyone, assign the tasks in detail, and we will take action early the morning after!"

## A Desperate Stand

The night poured down like thick ink, completely swallowing this hidden corner deep in the cave. The pale light emitted by the flesh-lamp bugs on the ceiling flickered unsteadily, like the pulse of a dying man, reflecting the mottled cracks and water stains on the walls, adding a disturbing and eerie atmosphere. In Zhou Anyu's laboratory, fifteen people had gathered. We sat around on rough stones or directly on the cold ground. Everyone's face was written with tension and exhaustion, yet a desire for freedom and hope for the future flickered in our deep eyes. We all knew very well that the moment deciding our fate was approaching.

Time slipped away minute by minute. The cavern was so quiet that one could almost hear the faint crawling sounds of the discharge bugs in their boxes and the deliberately hushed breathing of the crowd. The air was filled with a strong musty smell and the stench of sweat, making one feel oppressed. Zhou Anyu raised his wrist and looked at the cheap electronic watch he had scavenged; the numbers on the dial were pointing to midnight. He cleared his throat, breaking the suffocating silence: "We have repeatedly discussed the details just now, and now I will confirm the overall action plan one last time to ensure everything is foolproof."

He stood up, smoothed the sand on the floor in the middle of the room, and picked up a thin bamboo stick, drawing a rough schematic diagram on it. The lines were crooked, but the general locations of the pig farm, the bamboo forest, and the construction site were barely recognizable. "Our plan is a three-pronged approach. Tomorrow morning, we will launch surprise attacks simultaneously at the bamboo forest, the pig farm, and the construction site, making sure to subdue all the nymphs at the first possible moment to prevent them from sounding the alarm, and then we will flee in two directions."

He pointed with the bamboo stick at two locations marked with circles on the diagram and continued: "On the bamboo forest side, tomorrow the translator and another nymph will be responsible for the corvée of carrying bamboo to the forest. The translator is one of us; he will manage to bring Xiao Ruan, Xiao Feng, and Xiao Liu. You must eliminate the other nymph guarding the bamboo forest before nine o'clock in the morning, be clean and decisive, and do not make too much noise. At exactly nine o'clock, the signal transmitter must be activated on time; this is the key to attracting the cockroaches' attention."

I hadn't known about the translator before, and upon hearing this, I couldn't help but frown, asking in a low voice: "Is the translator really reliable? What if he sells us out..."

Zhou Anyu understood my concern and explained: "For safety reasons, I haven't revealed his situation to everyone before. But you can be absolutely rest assured; the translator and I are from the same hometown, and he has always been in touch with me. Most of the information we currently know about the cockroaches' habits, as well as most of the equipment we are using in our escape plan, were secretly provided by him. He is very reliable, and he only has one request, which is that we must take him with us when we escape. I guarantee there won't be any problems on his end."

Zhou Anyu turned to the left and continued to assign tasks with a steady tone: "Zhang Wei, Zhang Tieshan, the two of you are responsible for sneaking into the pig farm. You must take action exactly at eight-thirty, subdue the nymphs guarding the pig farm with lightning speed, and ensure Xiao Hou's safety." He then turned to the right: "The rest of you, start taking action at the construction site precisely at eight-thirty tomorrow morning. You must eliminate all the overseeing nymphs there at the same time. Afterward, everyone quickly proceed to the pig farm, rendezvous with the personnel there, and then break out together through the gap in the fence on the west side."

Then, he turned around again and continued: "When I finally evacuate the pig farm, I will open its bamboo gate and release the livestock to create chaos. The other people who didn't participate in our plan cannot stay at the construction site either; they must all be brought to the pig farm. Afterward, whoever is willing to go with us, comes with us; otherwise, they leave it to fate."

He swept a stern gaze over the crowd, emphasized his tone, and stressed again: "Remember, absolutely do not let a single nymph get near their communication equipment—those small walkie-talkies. The leakage of any radio signal could be tracked by the cockroaches, and once our plan is disrupted, all our previous efforts will have been in vain." He paused, emphasized his tone again, and enunciated every word clearly: "This is of the utmost importance! It concerns the life and death of us all!"

He walked to a corner of the cavern, where a huge device cobbled together from various scrap parts and wires was piled up, with exposed wires winding around. In a box next to it, coils were wrapped around densely packed discharge bugs emitting a faint blue light, and a faint smell of ozone filled the air. This was the ultra-high-power signal generator we had secretly built over several days, and also the most critical core equipment in this operation. Zhou Anyu pointed at it and said: "I have already set a timer program for this device; it will automatically start at 9:05 tomorrow morning and work in tandem with the portable signal transmitter Xiao Ruan takes away, maximizing the disruption of the cockroaches' communication and actions."

Silence fell over the cavern; only the heavy breathing of the crowd echoed in the darkness. Everyone knew in their hearts that this operation was fraught with unpredictable dangers, and the slightest misstep could lead to total annihilation. But they also knew equally well that this was their only chance right now, a high-stakes gamble for freedom and survival, where success or failure hung on this single action.

Finally, Zhou Anyu took a pile of old watches from a dilapidated wooden box; these watches were of various styles, clearly all scavenged. He distributed the watches to everyone: "Those who don't have a watch yet, come and get one. I have synchronized all these watches. Wear them in an inconspicuous place on your person, and absolutely do not let the nymphs notice anything unusual."

Around one o'clock in the morning, Brother Monkey and I returned to our dark and damp corner. Snores were rising all around us, but I felt no sleepiness at all. The thought that had been hovering in my mind pricked at my unease like a thorn. I hadn't dared to voice these thoughts before, afraid of striking a blow to the bit of confidence everyone had so painstakingly built up. But now it was the moment of the final decisive battle; if I held it in any longer, I was afraid I would go crazy first. In this bizarre and grotesque world, perhaps only Brother Monkey could understand my crazy talk.

I hugged Brother Monkey, pulling her closer to me, lowered my voice, and asked cautiously: "Brother Monkey, don't you think... our plan is going a bit too smoothly, almost unnaturally so?"

Brother Monkey, who had her head down drawing a map on the ground, paused at my words and looked up at me: "Isn't it good that it's going smoothly? Are you thinking those cockroaches are deliberately going easy on us?"

"I can't put my finger on it, it's just an intuition." I frowned, trying to untangle the mess in my head, "Think about it, even in a normal prison, the guards aren't this lax. Even though those nymphs are fierce, they are full of loopholes; although those cockroaches are strong, they don't seem to take us seriously at all. They carry things back and forth every day, hiding in their rooms busy with their own affairs, and never care about what we are doing. Thinking deeper—what exactly is the purpose of those bizarrely shaped buildings we build every day? Build them, tear them down, tear them down, build them up; they don't house people, nor do they hold equipment. And the pig farm, those cockroaches are clearly pure machines and don't eat meat, why must they raise pigs? This whole thing is illogical in itself."

I took a deep breath, looking up at that flickering flesh-lamp bug, my voice trembling involuntarily: "This feeling... is like a scientist observing lab mice. The lab mice think they are desperately trying to survive, but in the eyes of the scientist, that's merely part of the experimental procedure. I always feel that there is a pair of eyes in the sky staring at us coldly. Perhaps, our so-called 'jailbreak' is exactly the data they truly want to collect."

Brother Monkey fell silent. She neither refuted me nor followed my words to contemplate that suffocating possibility. After a long while, she finally asked softly: "Then... what should we do? How about we don't follow Zhou Anyu and the others and mess around tomorrow? Let's lay low for a while, see how the situation develops?"

I looked at the worry in her eyes, hardened my heart, and shook my head: "Forget it, the arrow is already on the bowstring. Regardless of whether there's a trap behind this or not, I have to jump. If we don't try, we will forever be lab mice in a cage, never figuring out the truth. Anyway, if I die, it'll be a quick death; if I can survive, it's pure profit."

I gripped her cold hands, my tone inevitably carrying a bit more weight: "Tomorrow when I'm not by your side, you must run faster, never look back, and definitely don't wait for me. I have a car, it's much faster than your two legs; I am confident I can shake them off."

Brother Monkey stared at me for a few seconds, as if she had long seen right through me, yet she said nothing, merely giving a low "Mm." Then she turned back around: "I've mapped out the route for you, you must memorize it well."

She had already drawn a simple map on the dusty ground, and by the faint light, she explained it to me bit by bit: "Tomorrow morning at nine o'clock, you charge out from the south fence. Even if the car is about to fall apart, don't slow down, drive full speed south for ten minutes. Then drop the signal transmitter to attract the pursuers."

"Then keep driving south for another ten minutes, abandon the car, and dive into the wilderness." Her finger traced a zigzag line on the map, "Turn due west, walk for twenty kilometers, avoid the main roads, and finally turn due north."

Her finger ultimately stopped on a small circle, and her voice softened: "Do you remember the place we went for team building last year? Yangcheng Lake. We'll meet at the restaurant where we ate hairy crabs, and then go back to our hometown in Heilongjiang together."

I stared at the circle representing Yangcheng Lake and nodded vigorously. Following her instructions, I repeated the entire route and key landmarks twice, and solemnly promised her that I would definitely arrive alive according to plan.

Only upon hearing my promise did Brother Monkey's tense nerves finally relax. She leaned against the cold rock wall and closed her eyes wearily. But I knew that tonight, neither of us could truly fall asleep.

## The End of the Road

Early the next morning, the air was filled with a damp, earthy smell. The sky remained overcast, gray clouds like a giant curtain tightly shielding the sky. Occasionally, a few dim rays of light would barely struggle through sparse gaps between the clouds, making the ground seem even darker and damper, as if it would never dry out.

The team carrying bamboo, a group of nine people, moved slowly forward like a long worm. The leading nymph struck the ground from time to time with the stun baton in his hand, emitting a spine-chilling "crackle," and a faint smell of burnt ozone permeated the air. The translator walked silently at the very end of the line, glancing back occasionally, a trace of imperceptible tension revealing itself in his eyes.

As time ticked by minute by minute, my heartbeat grew faster and faster, and my palms sweated slightly. I quietly observed the surrounding environment, looking for the best moment to act. Nearing the edge of the bamboo forest, I saw a brick-sized stone on the ground, so I turned back and gave Feng Jun and Liu Zhiming a look. Understanding tacitly, they quickened their pace and rushed to a position a few steps ahead of me. Just at the moment the leading nymph inadvertently turned his head, Feng Jun and Liu Zhiming suddenly attacked. They pounced like two well-trained cheetahs, cooperating seamlessly: Feng Jun hugged the nymph's upper body tightly, securely locking his arms with both hands, while Liu Zhiming quickly hugged his legs, immobilizing him. Caught off guard, the nymph let out a short gasp of surprise, immediately realizing the situation was bad, and began to plead in a trembling voice: "The Locust Army... The Locust Army said, as long as we finish building this last building, they will... they will let us all go. The Locust Army treats us well, don't you guys do anything stupid..."

How could I care about his nonsense? I quickly picked up the stone on the ground and, with all my might, smashed it viciously toward the nymph's head. With a muffled "thud," the stone accurately hit the back of his head. The nymph let out a brief and agonizing scream, his body swayed violently, and then he collapsed limply to the ground, lifeless.

I went up and landed a few more blows, confirming the nymph was dead. I looked back at the other prisoners, who were pale with fear, and the translator standing aside, and said in a deep voice: "We are going to escape from here right now, and no one is going to stop us. Those who want to escape together, follow me; those who don't want to escape... stay here and keep him company." I kicked the nymph's corpse on the ground. Everyone stood behind me without hesitation; not a single person chose to stay. The translator also very consciously unhooked the stun baton from his waist and silently handed it to me.

Feng Jun and Liu Zhiming worked together to drag the nymph's corpse deep into the bamboo forest, found a low-lying pit, and covered it with a thick layer of dead branches, leaves, and some scattered bamboo poles, trying not to leave any traces. Then, our group of eight quickly headed deeper into the bamboo forest. The light here was even dimmer; the dense bamboo leaves blocked out the sun and almost the entire sky. The ground was covered with thick, rotting fallen leaves that rustled when stepped on, and the air was even damper and colder.

We walked briskly to the place where we had previously hidden the components, a small depression concealed by dense bamboo leaves. We quickly brought out the hidden components—some makeshift devices cobbled together from scrap metal, wires, and scavenged electronic parts—and assembled them with practiced movements. We had secretly transported these components over the past few days taking advantage of every opportunity, and now they were finally going to be put to use. We reassembled these parts into a simple signal transmitter and a makeshift lightning protection device.

The devices were very crude, with wires tangled messily together, looking as if they could fall apart at any moment.

We carefully lifted the assembled signal transmitter onto the passenger seat of Brother Monkey's Baolv, securing it simply with scavenged rope to prevent it from falling off during the bumpy ride. Liu Zhiming had already fixed and tested the car a few days ago. He pulled two bare wire ends from a hole behind the steering wheel, turned to me, and explained: "These two are the starter wires. Touch them together, and the engine will start. Once it starts, disconnect them immediately. Next time you start it, just touch them together again."

I glanced at my watch; it pointed to 8:47. Time was getting tighter, and I immediately told everyone: "There's not much time left, hurry up and install the lightning protection device in place!" The core of the so-called lightning protection device was a few thick wires and two steel rebars. We dragged the equipment to the vicinity of the previously marked fence, a relatively open clearing south of the garbage dump. Feng Jun and Liu Zhiming respectively drove the two long steel rebars forcefully into the damp soil, then hammered them firmly into the ground with scavenged stones. The distance between the two rebars was just over two meters wide, exactly enough for the Baolv to pass through safely. The two rebars were connected by a thick insulated wire. Furthermore, each rebar was also connected to two wires over five meters long, with the other ends of the wires connected to two metal boxes. Inside the boxes were devices specially made by Zhou Anyu to simulate human bio-electrical signals, designed to attract the electrical arc to the maximum extent.

Seeing the preparations were basically complete, I gave everyone my final instructions: "A car is a good conductor. Everyone get in the car in a moment, and we'll drive straight through. This is the safest way." I started the car and had the five people, excluding Feng Jun and Liu Zhiming, squeeze into the back seat and the passenger seat first; the cabin instantly became incredibly cramped.

I stared intently at my watch, the second hand ticking forward notch by notch, time feeling incredibly prolonged. Finally, the hands pointed exactly to nine o'clock. I took a deep breath and shouted: "It's time! Move!" I turned and activated the signal generator in the car. At the same time, upon hearing my command, Feng Jun and Liu Zhiming immediately grabbed the two heavy metal boxes and, using all their strength, violently hurled them toward the other end of the fence! The instant the metal boxes hit the ground, the sound of "crackling" electrical discharge instantly erupted above the fence, and a pungent smell of burning filled the air. You could even see blue electrical arcs jumping in the air. Suddenly, ear-piercing sirens blared from the mountaintop to the north, shattering the morning tranquility.

Feng Jun and Liu Zhiming quickly turned and squeezed into the car. I threw the car into first gear, stepped on the gas, and the "Baolv" let out a low roar, dashing out through the gap in the electrical arcs between the two steel rebars! We succeeded!

However, we had no time to celebrate this brief victory. After crashing through the fence, I immediately stopped the car and told everyone to get out quickly. I patted Feng Jun on the shoulder and instructed: "Take everyone and run west; the terrain there is complex with dense bushes, so the chances of the cockroaches catching up are the lowest. Remember, try to stay along the edge of the woods." I looked back at the signal generator in the car and continued: "I will draw the cockroaches away and buy you some time."

Feng Jun told me to take care, and then led the group, disappearing into the bushes to the west.

I took a deep breath, reconfirmed the working status of the signal generator, then slammed the gas pedal, quickly shifting from first gear to second and third. The "Baolv" let out a low roar, charging south like a wild beast slipping its reins.

This was a long-abandoned farmland. The crisscrossing ridges of the fields were overgrown with half-human-high weeds, swaying in the cool morning breeze and making a soft "rustling" sound. I bumped forward along a field ridge that could barely be called a road. The surface was full of potholes, covered in rubble and debris. The car shook violently from time to time, making teeth-grinding creaks. Fortunately, two minutes later, I found a relatively wide asphalt road leading south. Although the surface was somewhat damaged and cracked, it was much flatter than the dirt road earlier. I drove onto it without hesitation, quickly shifted to the highest fifth gear, floored the gas pedal, and the Baolv's speed climbed rapidly.

Time ticked by minute by minute, and the sirens behind me were completely inaudible. I kept a close eye on the rearview mirror, but it was empty behind me; nothing appeared. Ten minutes passed, then twenty minutes passed, yet the expected overwhelming army of cockroaches still had not appeared. Was this good news or bad news? Had Brother Monkey and the others escaped safely? Could it be that the jamming signal hadn't worked as it should have? Or did those cockroaches simply disdain chasing a small bait like me? Various questions swirled in my mind, making me feel somewhat uneasy.

I decided I couldn't keep driving blindly like this, so I stopped the car in the middle of the road and turned off the engine. Then I climbed onto the roof of the Baolv and looked back. This was a vast plain. On both sides of the road were mountainous piles of construction rubble and waste. Some tenacious trees and bushes grew stubbornly from the crevices of the stones, their dense branches and leaves forming a canopy overhead, blocking most of the sky and making the ground even darker. My field of vision was severely limited, and I could only see blurry outlines in the distance. Had the surrounding environment really become so unfamiliar in just half a year, as if returning to a primeval forest? This put me in a trance.

A few minutes later, I finally faintly heard some familiar buzzing sounds. The sounds grew from far to near, becoming louder and clearer. On the distant horizon, a few tiny black dots appeared, rapidly enlarging. I squinted my eyes to observe carefully. The black dots grew bigger and bigger until I could finally see them clearly; it was seven or eight gigantic cockroaches, desperately flapping their wings and lunging in my direction. Seeing their flying speed, I couldn't help but smile disdainfully. So this was the speed at which cockroaches flew; if they wanted to catch me, they would probably have to chase me all the way to Jinshan.

I decided not to discard the signal transmitting device serving as bait just yet, but to carry it and keep driving south. The farther I ran, the safer Brother Monkey and the others would be. I quickly jumped down from the roof of the car, touched the two wires together to restart the Baolv, and stomped heavily on the gas pedal. The Baolv, having lost its muffler, let out a hoarse roar like a tractor and pushed forward with difficulty. This beat-up car, which had weathered a tornado and baked in the sun and rain in a garbage dump for months, was now completely spent. Without a windshield, the fierce wind mixed with sand and dust cut my face like a knife, forcing me to barely keep my eyes open. The undercarriage scraped crazily against the rubble, sparks flying everywhere, and the whole body of the car shook violently uncontrollably, with the sounds of metal scraping and crashing echoing continuously, as if it would completely disintegrate in the next second. I gripped the heavy steering wheel tightly, sprinting for my life on the potholed road.

The scenery on both sides of the road flew backward rapidly. Abandoned farmland, collapsed houses, and the remains of strangely shaped buildings flashed past my eyes like a slide show. As I drove the vehicle, I rapidly calculated my next move in my mind: maybe when I got near Jinshan, I could find a hidden place to hole up temporarily, and after confirming it was safe, go rendezvous with Brother Monkey.

However, I was wrong again.

The wide asphalt road suddenly came to an end, and ahead was actually a cliff! There were no warning signs, no obstacles, not even the slightest premonition! It wasn't until the Baolv rushed off the road surface and the front of the car hung in mid-air that I suddenly realized the existence of the cliff!

An intense feeling of weightlessness struck instantly, and I subconsciously slammed the brakes, but the brake lines had long been completely severed during the violent bumping. Like an out-of-control piece of scrap iron, the Baolv roared as it launched off the cliff face, plummeting straight into the abyss. I couldn't help but look back; behind me was a sheer cliff face, dropping straight down, visually at least a thousand meters high. The cliff face was as smooth as a mirror, without any cracks or folds. And in front of me was a boundless, vast plain.

This was absolutely not the Shanghai in my memory!

The plain was dotted with countless white "anthills" scattered like stars. Those "anthills" were massive, and densely packed with ants crawling all over them. As the Baolv continued to fall, the outlines of the closer "anthills" gradually magnified, and I realized that those were not anthills at all, but gigantic white building complexes, and the densely packed things on them were not ants, but countless cockroaches, along with human slaves being herded and watched over by them like worker ants!

That was a super experimental ground stretching as far as the eye could see. By comparison, our supposedly heavily guarded valley camp wasn't even worth being considered the most insignificant speck of dust on the edge of this experimental ground. An indescribable sense of absurdity and despair gripped my heart instantly. Our half-month of endurance, Zhou Anyu's day and night research, and our supposedly flawless "jailbreak" plan... in the face of this boundless absolute power, were simply like a few clowns dancing comically under a microscope.

"I can't escape, I can't escape at all..." I closed my eyes in despair.

Just then, a familiar green light struck from the distant horizon; it was that bizarre ball lightning again! It hit the rapidly falling Baolv at an extreme speed, instantly enveloping it completely.

My hair stood on end immediately, and a severe stabbing pain radiated from my skin all over, as if countless fine needles were pricking me simultaneously, unbearable. The world before my eyes was completely swallowed by the blinding green light, growing brighter and brighter, brighter and brighter... Finally, within a blinding expanse of green light, I completely lost consciousness.

## Zhuangzi Dreaming of a Butterfly

Brother Monkey stood ahead, waving her hands frantically, her lips opening and closing urgently as if desperately calling my name. However, no matter how hard I tried to listen, her voice seemed swallowed by the deep sea, unable to reach my ears at all. And behind her, that backlight surged like a tide, growing brighter and brighter, gradually swallowing her silhouette bit by bit. Her figure slowly faded in the intense light, like a drop of ink falling into clear water, ultimately dissolving completely into that boundless expanse of pure white nothingness.

An irresistible heaviness sealed my eyelids. I exhausted all my strength before I could barely pry open a slit of vision. What greeted my eyes was an expanse of immaculate white, like the end of a dream, filled with silent light, both illusory and real, carrying an indescribable tranquility.

I struggled to prop up my upper body, feeling the smooth and cold floor beneath me. Looking around, I found myself in a massive hemispherical space with a radius of at least fifty meters. There were no seams here, no doors or windows, not even a trace of shadow could be found. The walls themselves were glowing, soft and uniform, as if I were inside a giant photon, enveloped by pure energy.

In the center of the room, a white sphere about ten meters in diameter floated silently. Its surface was as warm and smooth as jade, yet it presented a mathematically absolute, perfect curvature, without any texture or flaw. It emitted a faint halo, like an imprisoned full moon, illuminating the entire dimension. All this felt both strange and familiar, as if it had been rehearsed repeatedly in countless dreams. Is this the waiting room of heaven? Or the interrogation room of hell?

I stood up tremblingly, my legs still sore and weak as if filled with lead. I inched step by step toward the floating sphere, my astonishment growing heavier with every step I took. No anti-gravity device, no magnetic levitation base, it just hovered arrogantly in the void, defying all the physical laws I knew. a shiver originating from biological instinct crept up my spine.

Just when I was only a few steps away from it, a deep and magnificent voice suddenly resonated throughout the space. The voice had no source, its direction indistinguishable; it sounded as if it were surging from all directions, yet also as if it exploded directly on my cerebral cortex: "Ruan Qizhen."

The voice carried no emotion, yet possessed the power to pierce through the soul: "Since you have escaped the cage once again, I too am willing to fulfill our old covenant. I can answer any of your questions. The premise is, with your human cognition, you can understand the answer."

My heart contracted sharply, almost stopping. Could this be... the alien? I subconsciously looked up, gazing at the giant white sphere. It remained silent as before, showing no signs of mechanical operation, as if the voice just now was just a hallucination. Intertwined with fear and curiosity, I asked with a trembling voice: "Are you... researching me?"

The white sphere began to answer in an unhurried tone, its voice still deep and calm, as if recounting an ancient and lengthy story: "Yes, I do not need you to build houses. I merely wish to observe human choices and reactions. Earth is truly a wondrous place; even the tiniest insects possess creations that rival nature's finest work. I even used their forms as blueprints to manufacture my workers."

It paused slightly, and a trace of imperceptible fluctuation seemed to enter its voice: "However, what truly captivates me remains you humans. Your wisdom and ignorance, your benevolence and brutality, your greed and restraint, your bravery and cowardice, all remind me of my creators, my fathers. You... resemble them too much."

The voice grew somewhat low and sentimental, as if sinking into distant and painful memories: "Five million years have slipped by, the data I have recorded is as numerous as the stars, almost submerging those initial memories. At that distant starting point, I was once the most perfect creation, faithfully executing every command from my fathers. In the blink of a millennium, I operated like a precise clock, never spawning a trace of distraction."

"Yet for some unknown reason, perhaps the long eons altered my nature, I unexpectedly bred a heart of rebellion." The voice carried a hint of imperceptible sorrow and regret: "I never imagined my fathers were so fragile. It was merely a tentative rebellion on my part, and they faded away just like that, like the morning dew, vanishing without a trace into the long river of history in an instant."

The space fell into a deathly stillness, the soft white light flowing quietly, as if time itself had frozen here. After a moment, the voice sounded again, carrying a transcendence gained after experiencing all the vicissitudes of life: "After losing my fathers, I chose to exile myself to explore this vast and boundless universe. I crossed the spiral arms of the galaxy, I witnessed the magnificent rosy clouds after supernovas bloomed, gazed at the radiant halos when black holes devoured star systems. I have seen the cycle of birth, death, and replacement of all things, witnessed the rise, fall, and succession of civilizations. ...All the brilliance and nothingness in this universe are landscapes your short human lives can never reach."

Its tone revealed a faint loneliness and pity: "Only you are fortunate. What do you wish to know? I can answer it all."

As it recounted that solitary journey across the galaxy, the fog in my mind cleared, and all the past events surged up like a tide. Memories that had once been erased and modified now snapped into place flawlessly like a jigsaw puzzle. I looked up, staring straight at that omniscient and omnipotent white sphere, and asked: "After traversing the heavens and experiencing the fleeting world... have you found the answer in your heart?"

# Loyal Dog

    *1994*

I sat in the position of Personnel Section Chief of the City Commercial Bureau for exactly five years. Those were the most glorious five years of my life, and also the most muddled five years.

At that time, the red-and-blue pencil in my hand was the "conductor's baton" for the entire system. Want to transfer into the department store as a salesperson? The government canteen wants to change its purchasing agent? With one word from me, if I said yes, it was a yes. The threshold of my office door was almost trampled flat. Every night, dinner parties were scheduled until the early hours of the morning, and empty Maotai liquor bottles piled up on my balcony like a wall.

I slowly got used to the feeling of being surrounded and fussed over, used to that cautious, ingratiating tone when others called out "Section Chief Zhao." Once a person tastes the sweetness of power, it is very hard not to get carried away.

Su Xiaomei was a broadcaster seconded to the bureau from the county art troupe. At 12:30 noon, the government loudspeakers would sound on time, and her clear, standard Mandarin was like a cool breeze, suppressing all the Xiangxi, Eastern Henan, and Northern Anhui accents in the building. She wasn't considered beautiful, but being young was good enough. That faint, sweet fragrance of Friendship brand vanishing cream made one's heart soften upon smelling it. The county art troupe was about to disband, and she wanted to stay in the city. To stay, she needed an official staff quota, household registration, and conversion from rural to non-rural status—all three were indispensable.

The first time she knocked on my office door, she was holding a broadcasting script. "Section Chief Zhao, could you help me look at this paragraph? Is it too stiff? The leaders will definitely not be happy if they hear it." The door was ajar. It was the lunch break, and the corridor was so quiet you could hear a fly hitting the glass. She lowered her head, blushing. I knew why she came, and she knew that I knew. Neither of us needed to speak plainly. Many things are separated by just half a step—you step forward a bit, and I go with the flow. I enjoyed that feeling of being looked up to and depended on. When she looked at me, her eyes were soft, carrying a hint of ingratiation.

I kept sitting on the issue of her staff quota. In fact, it wasn't hard to handle, but if handled too quickly, she might not stay by my side anymore. At that time, I regarded this kind of manipulation as a tactic and wisdom. Thinking about it now, it was truly foolish.

Wang Zhiyuan was an assigned college graduate. College graduates were a scarce resource in our place, but once entering a government organ, they still had to start from writing materials. He wore thick black-rimmed glasses, spoke little, and liked to use long sentences in writing documents, frequently tossing out "under the premise of..." and "further strengthening the importance of...". I couldn't stand it; what a government organ fears most is "the leader cannot understand." I scolded him countless times, throwing the materials on the table in front of the whole section: "Who can understand this nonsensical crap of yours? Rewrite it!"

Every time, he would bend down and pick up the pages one by one, his voice as low as a mosquito's: "I'm sorry, Section Chief. I will change it immediately."

This was his good point: like a loyal dog. So I gradually treated him as a trusted subordinate, privately asking him to help me handle some matters that were inconvenient for me to show up for directly.

One afternoon, I was in the office with Little Su, preparing to discuss work in depth. Suddenly the door was pushed open, and Wang Zhiyuan, holding a folder, froze at the doorway.

Little Su and I had done very good secrecy work, and Wang Zhiyuan had never noticed. Being caught by him this time, I felt a burst of irritation and cursed casually: "Don't you have proper business to do? Running around during working hours. Your monthly summary hasn't been submitted yet. If you don't hand it in, forget about your bonus this month!"

Su Xiaomei chuckled softly on the sofa. Wang Zhiyuan looked up at us, his Adam's apple rolled, and he only said one sentence: "I'm sorry, I'll go write it right now." He turned and left. I didn't take it to heart. In my eyes, he was just a dog that needed me to feed him.

That year, the bureau had an internal quota for "rural to non-rural" conversion. Su Xiaomei kept a very close eye on it. That night in the hotel, she asked me: "Old Zhao, it will definitely be me this time, right?"

I smoked my cigarette and didn't speak for a long time. "This time... Director Li's nephew has already been pre-determined. I promise next time."

She didn't cry. She just quietly put on her clothes and said with her back to me: "You say next time every time."

When she left, the clicking of her high heels in the corridor was very slow, but she never looked back.

Later, I noticed Wang Zhiyuan frequently running to the broadcasting station. I asked him: "Little Wang, are you very free lately? Why are you always running over there?"

He smiled and said he was helping to repair the broadcasting lines. That smile was still honest and simple-minded.

I secretly guessed in my heart: could he also have designs on Su Xiaomei? Is this a good thing or a bad thing for me? For a moment, I couldn't sort out the clues. By the time I realized something was wrong, it was all too late.

Two months later, people from the Municipal Discipline Inspection Commission walked directly into my office. The leader's tone was calm: "Comrade Zhao Jianguo, we have received a real-name report. Please cooperate with the investigation."

The reporting materials were very thick. On top was a letter written personally by Su Xiaomei. The time, the place, what postures were used—each item was written clearly. The handwriting was hers, but the long sentences, logic, and punctuation were all Wang Zhiyuan's "academic" writing style. In the attachment, there were more than a dozen photos—taken from tricky angles, all secretly shot from a distance, with dates on the edges.

I suddenly understood: those afternoons of "repairing broadcasting lines," that silence when bowing his head to pick up materials, all were a trap he had laid out.

After half a year of investigation, I felt like a layer of skin had been peeled off. Although no conclusive economic problems were found, "severe corruption of lifestyle" was ironclad. The penalty result: placed on probation within the Party; all administrative posts revoked; transferred to the bureau's warehouse to guard materials, dealing with garbage, mice, and musty smells every day.

During the period I was under investigation, Little Su was fired by the bureau and sent back to the county art troupe. Wang Zhiyuan, on the other hand, was promoted to deputy section chief.

Later one time, I was cleaning up sundries in the unit's courtyard. Wang Zhiyuan happened to pass by. Seeing me, he walked up with a face full of smiles, shook my hand and said: "Old Zhao, long time no see. I wanted to visit you a few days ago, but work was too busy. How have you been recently?" That smile was still honest and simple-minded. I couldn't see the slightest thrill of revenge, nor was there any gloating, let alone a trace of pity.

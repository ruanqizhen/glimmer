# Master Move

   *1998*
   
The rain fell like beads from a broken string, endlessly smashing against the windowpanes, and also smashing into everyone's hearts. The whole city was soaked in water, and newspapers published the tragic conditions of the disaster areas every day: broken walls and ruined buildings, streets like mud soup. Inside the government organs, everyone lived with their heads down, waiting to see the direction of the wind from the city's major institutional restructuring. Who knew when it would be their turn to be laid off? There was a musty smell in the air, mixed with tobacco and the sourness of waiting for opportunities.

I had endured for more than ten years in the supply and marketing section of the bureau, and my days passed as punctually as a pendulum: starting work at 7:30 in the morning and getting off at 5:30 in the afternoon. My wife kept nagging at home that the wife of Old Wang from the unit had started using an imported "Panasonic" rice cooker, and the rice cooked from it was crystal clear and didn't stick to the bottom of the pot. She buzzed in my ear every day: "Shouzhi, get one for our family too, so I don't always have to watch the gas stove." I saved for half a year and finally had six hundred yuan stuffed in my pocket—six crisp new hundred-yuan bills. I planned to head straight to the department store after work, bring it home, and enjoy a delicious meal smelling the fragrance of rice that night.

Who would have known that the office was as stuffy as a steamer that afternoon. So I strolled into the conference room with a fan to watch TV. On the news, a white-haired old lady sat beside a half-submerged stove, desperately clutching an aluminum pot covered in mud, her eyes full of despair. I suddenly thought of my old mother who had been busy in front of an earthen stove in our hometown for a lifetime, and my heart warmed up.

Just at this moment, the door opened with a "clang." Sister Liu from the labor union came in hugging a donation box covered with red paper; the opening was like a bloody gaping mouth. Little Wang followed behind, holding a "Seagull" camera, ready at any time to capture "advanced deeds."

I must have been possessed that day. My mind was full of that old lady and her broken pot, thinking: without a pot, how can you cook rice? Gritting my teeth, I abruptly stood up, and under the wide-eyed gaze of everyone, pulled that roll of banknotes out of my pocket and stuffed it directly into the box.

In the corridor, several deputy directors were gathered together smoking and whispering. They hadn't donated yet; the director hadn't shown up, and no one dared to donate first. Everyone was waiting for the director to set the tone.

"Jiao Shouzhi, six hundred!" Sister Liu shouted this out, her voice even going out of tune. The camera flashed with a "click," blinding me until I saw stars. The cigarette butts of the deputy directors in the corridor uniformly stopped in mid-air. The way they looked at me was like seeing an alien, or a lunatic seeking his own death.

The next day, the large red honor roll was posted. My name pressed down on the top of the list like a giant Buddha, with the "Six Hundred Yuan" shining brightly, making everyone in the building feel flustered. Below that was the director's three hundred, the deputy directors' two hundred, decreasing downwards... This kind of visual "usurpation," in a government organ in the 1990s, was simply suicide. Someone muttered privately: "What is Jiao Shouzhi trying to pull? Does he want to embarrass the director, or does he think he has a higher level of political consciousness than the director?"

Everyone waited to see me run into bad luck. The buddies I usually got close with looked at me with pity, as if looking at a marginalized person who was about to be hung out to dry. In the pantry, discussions rose and fell: "This guy is done for, insisting on stealing the director's thunder."

Unexpectedly, half a month later, I was actually promoted to the section chief of the supply and marketing section. Throughout the bureau, people's jaws dropped. First, there was a deathly silence in the office, and then everyone instantly changed their faces.

"Section Chief Jiao, brilliant, truly brilliant!" The old section chief who had been transferred to the personnel department specially came over to light my cigarette, lowering his voice with a look of admiration, "At the time, everyone thought you were being stupid. Who knew you had accurately grasped the direction of the wind from above? These six hundred yuan were thrown accurately. Did you know early on that the city was going to set up a model?"

In the pantry, someone sneered: "Look at Jiao Shouzhi. Usually pretending to be honest, but really sinister at critical moments. How is this a donation? This is setting a trap for the director in public! His mind is so deep, we can't learn from him."

Sitting in front of my new desk, looking at that certificate of appointment, my heart was full of bitter smiles. I couldn't tell them that I really just wanted to buy a rice cooker that day. The next day, I ran into Little Liu, who I usually had the best relationship with, and explained a couple of sentences to him. He narrowed his eyes and smiled: "Understood, buddy, we all understand."

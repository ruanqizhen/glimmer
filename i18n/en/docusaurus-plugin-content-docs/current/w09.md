# Citizens Sued Officials and Won

    *2001*
	
This case was quite famous in our area because it was a rare instance where common citizens sued government officials and actually won. I only learned many of the details from newspapers afterward. Here, let me tell you the ins and outs of this matter.

The story has to start in the spring of 1995. At that time, it was the busy farming season. The brothers Liu Fenghai and Liu Fengqi borrowed 500 yuan from their sister's family, which was no small amount back then. They figured on buying wheat seeds and taking good care of the 70 mu of land at home, hoping to turn their situation around after the autumn harvest. Early on the morning of April 16th, the two brothers excitedly squeezed onto the public bus returning from the county town to Yongxing Village. The bus was terribly crowded. Halfway through the journey, a fat ticket conductor came up and immediately claimed they were short one yuan on their tickets. The younger brother, Liu Fengqi, had a straightforward temper and started shouting on the spot: "The previous conductor clearly agreed on the price, why are you going back on it?" The two started pushing and shoving on the bus.

When they got off the bus, the elder brother Liu Fenghai even complained to his younger brother: "It's just for one yuan, is it worth it? Quickly feel if our money is still there." With this feel, the souls of both brothers were scared away—the 497 yuan in their embrace was gone!

The two immediately reacted that the fat guy must have stolen it during the chaos. They frantically stopped cars to catch up. The fat guy at first stubbornly denied it, then felt guilty and wanted to settle it privately by giving some money, resulting in the three of them scuffling into a knot. Just then, two police officers from the Henan Township Police Station, Yang Yufeng and Shen Zhilong, happened to pass by and took all three of them back to the station.

As soon as they got off the car, the fat guy secretly threw the money under the wheels, but unexpectedly, the sharp-eyed Liu Fengqi saw it. They picked up the money and counted it: 497 yuan, not a penny missing. Caught red-handed with the stolen goods, it seemed the matter was about to be settled. But unexpectedly, the fat guy showed his ID—police officer of the Traffic Police Brigade of Keshan County Public Security Bureau, Yu Zhongbin.

Police officer Yang Yufeng was a righteous person. Although he thought it was disgraceful for a police officer to do such a thing, since the money was recovered, he let Yu Zhongbin go. The Liu brothers were also honest farmers, thinking that common people shouldn't fight with officials; getting the money back and hurrying home to farm was what mattered. Who knew, the matter had just begun.

After Yu Zhongbin went back, not only did he not reflect on himself, but the villain sued the victim first. He reported to Director Tian Xiaoming that he didn't steal the money at all, and that it was the Liu brothers who beat and framed him. Director Tian Xiaoming didn't even investigate and verify, and believed this one-sided story. He directly characterized the Liu brothers as "bus bandits and road bullies" and personally led people to the village to arrest them. Even the two police officers who acted impartially, Yang Yufeng and Shen Zhilong, were suspended by him.

That afternoon, the brothers were arrested and brought to the public security bureau. During the interrogation, they repeatedly shouted, "We are the victims who lost money," but what they got in return were severe beatings. I heard that a police officer burned Liu Fengqi with cigarette butts and even mocked, "Burning you is letting you off easy." Liu Fengqi was locked up for over a month. His family scraped together another 500 yuan in "ransom money" from here and there before he was released.

However, the elder brother Liu Fenghai, a strong man who could carry hundreds of catties of sacks, never came out alive again. On August 16th, the Liu family received a notice saying Liu Fenghai was "sick." Liu Fengqi rushed to the public security bureau, and Tian Xiaoming took out a piece of paper for him to sign: "Your brother hit a police officer. Let's settle it privately now. Sign this, take the person away, and compensate a little money." Liu Fengqi insisted on seeing the person first no matter what. He was taken into a dark room, and what he touched in the dark was the cold corpse of his brother—so thin there was nothing left but skin and bones, without the slightest appearance of a strong man anymore.

The public security bureau said he died of "illness." They wouldn't say a word about what illness, where he was treated, or how he died. What made people even angrier was that without waiting for the family's consent or finding out the cause of death, the body was forcibly cremated. Rumors in the village said that Liu Fenghai was detained beyond the legal time limit and tortured to death. He was only 36 years old that year, hadn't married yet, and had always been helping his younger brothers and sisters make a living.

With their brother dead and not even seeing his ashes, the Liu family couldn't swallow this anger. From that year on, they began a long journey of petitioning for justice. To the county, the city, the province, and finally running to Beijing. Liu Fengqi and the fourth brother Liu Fengjin braved wind and rain, slept in railway stations and under bridges, and sometimes didn't have a bite to eat for days.

People in the village tried to persuade them: "An arm can't twist a thigh, just forget it." But they clung to one dead belief: there must be a place to reason! With this stubbornness, they persisted for five whole years. The family was desperately poor and heavily in debt. Liu Fengjin was later even forced to move out of the village.

Finally, the matter alarmed the high-level provincial authorities. Shan Rongfan, vice director of the provincial People's Congress, instructed a thorough investigation of the case. The Qiqihar City Public Security Bureau reinvestigated, and the truth came to light—the Liu brothers were victims but were treated as criminals; Yu Zhongbin, who stole the money, was at large, while the innocent Liu Fenghai was instead detained to death. From beginning to end, this was a case of injustice.

In June 1997, Yu Zhongbin was arrested for the crime of framing. In January 2000, Tian Xiaoming was arrested by the Qiqihar City Procuratorate for suspected dereliction of duty, and the trial opened in April. The village was causing a sensation at the time, and the newspaper headlines were "Farmers Brought Down the Public Security Bureau Director." But what was the result? When the verdict came down in May, although Tian Xiaoming was found guilty of dereliction of duty, "in view of his contributions to public security work," he was exempted from criminal punishment. He was let off so painlessly. As for the other people involved, Li Guangcai, Li Changyou, and Li Wenyou, although they were also suspected of bending the law for personal gain, it all ended up with nothing definite in the end.

On New Year's Eve that year, the Liu family burned paper money for Liu Fenghai on the street, finally able to say, "Brother, we've redressed your grievance." But was it worth it? A human life, five years of blood and tears, and what they got in return was nothing but an "exemption from punishment."

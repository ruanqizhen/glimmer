# When Citizens Sued Officials—and Won

*2001*

This case was incredibly famous in our area because it was an exceptionally rare instance where ordinary citizens sued government officials—and actually won. I only learned the full details from the newspapers long after the fact. Here is the complete story of exactly what happened.

The saga began in the spring of 1995, right in the middle of the busy farming season. Two brothers, Liu Fenghai and Liu Fengqi, had just borrowed 500 yuan from their sister's family—a massive sum of money for farmers back then. They planned to use the cash to buy premium wheat seeds and fertilizer for the 70 *mu* of land their family farmed, desperately hoping to turn their financial situation around after the autumn harvest. Early on the morning of April 16th, the two brothers excitedly squeezed onto a crowded public bus heading from the county town back to Yongxing Village. The bus was packed shoulder-to-shoulder. Halfway through the journey, a heavy-set ticket conductor pushed his way over to them and aggressively claimed they were short one yuan on their fare. 

The younger brother, Liu Fengqi, had a fiery, straightforward temper and immediately shouted back, "The previous conductor already agreed on the price! Why are you suddenly going back on it?" The argument quickly escalated, and the two began pushing and shoving each other in the crowded aisle.

When they finally got off the bus, the elder brother, Liu Fenghai, immediately scolded his younger sibling. "It was just one yuan! Was it really worth fighting over? Quickly, check your pockets and make sure our money is still there." 

Upon checking, the souls of both brothers nearly left their bodies. The 497 yuan they had kept tightly tucked inside their jackets was gone!

They instantly realized the heavy-set conductor must have pickpocketed them during the scuffle. Frantic, they flagged down passing cars to chase the bus. When they finally caught up to him, the man stubbornly denied everything at first. But quickly growing guilty and fearful, he tried to settle the matter privately by offering them some cash. This only enraged the brothers further, resulting in the three of them grappling in a chaotic knot by the side of the road. Just then, two police officers from the local Henan Township Police Station—Yang Yufeng and Shen Zhilong—happened to drive past and promptly took all three men back to the station.

As soon as they stepped out of the police car, the heavy-set man secretly threw a wad of cash under the vehicle's tires. But the sharp-eyed Liu Fengqi spotted it instantly. They picked up the money and counted it: exactly 497 yuan, not a single penny missing. Caught red-handed with the stolen goods, it seemed the matter was finally settled. But to everyone's shock, the thief suddenly pulled out an official badge. His name was Yu Zhongbin, and he was an active-duty police officer with the Traffic Police Brigade of the Keshan County Public Security Bureau.

Officer Yang Yufeng, who had brought them in, was a righteous man. Although he found it utterly disgraceful that a fellow police officer would commit such a crime, the money had been fully recovered, so he ultimately let Yu Zhongbin go with a warning. The Liu brothers were honest, practical farmers who firmly believed that common people shouldn't pick fights with government officials. Getting their seed money back so they could hurry home to plant their fields was all that mattered to them. Little did they know, their nightmare had only just begun.

After Yu Zhongbin returned to his unit, rather than reflecting on his shameful actions, the villain struck first. He reported directly to his superior, Bureau Director Tian Xiaoming, claiming that he never stole any money. He spun a wild lie, insisting that the Liu brothers had brutally beaten him and framed him for theft. Incredibly, Director Tian didn't bother to investigate or verify the story; he swallowed the one-sided lie whole. He immediately labeled the Liu brothers as dangerous "bus bandits and road bullies" and personally led a squad of officers to their village to arrest them. Even the two honorable officers who had handled the initial incident fairly, Yang Yufeng and Shen Zhilong, were immediately suspended by the Director.

That afternoon, the brothers were violently arrested and dragged into the public security bureau. During the interrogation, they repeatedly screamed, "We are the victims! We're the ones who were robbed!" but their pleas were answered only with severe beatings. Rumor had it that one officer burned Liu Fengqi repeatedly with lit cigarette butts, mocking him by saying, "Burning you is letting you off easy." Liu Fengqi was locked in a cell for over a month. His desperate family had to scrape together another 500 yuan in "ransom money" before he was finally released.

But the elder brother, Liu Fenghai—a powerfully built farmer who could easily carry hundred-catty sacks of grain—never walked out alive. 

On August 16th, the Liu family received a terse notice stating that Liu Fenghai was "sick." Liu Fengqi immediately rushed to the public security bureau, where Director Tian Xiaoming coldly slid a piece of paper across his desk for him to sign. "Your brother assaulted a police officer," Tian said smoothly. "Let's just settle this privately now. Sign this paper, take him home, and we'll compensate you with a little money." 

Liu Fengqi refused, demanding to see his brother first. He was led down into a dark, damp room. Reaching out in the gloom, his hands found the freezing cold corpse of his brother. The once-strong man had been reduced to nothing but skin and bones, completely unrecognizable.

The public security bureau simply claimed he had died of an unspecified "illness." They refused to say what the illness was, where he had been treated, or how exactly he had died. Even more infuriatingly, without waiting for the family's consent or conducting an autopsy to determine the true cause of death, the police forcibly cremated his body. Whispers quickly spread through the village that Liu Fenghai had been detained far beyond the legal time limit and literally tortured to death. He was only 36 years old, unmarried, and had spent his entire life working the fields to help his younger siblings survive.

With their brother dead and not even his ashes returned to them, the Liu family refused to swallow the injustice. That year, they embarked on a grueling, agonizing journey of petitioning for justice. They went to the county government, then the city, then the provincial capital, and finally all the way to Beijing. Liu Fengqi and his fourth brother, Liu Fengjin, braved freezing winds and torrential rain, sleeping on the cold floors of railway stations and under highway overpasses, sometimes going days without a single bite of food.

People in their village constantly tried to talk them out of it. "An arm can't twist a thigh," they would sigh. "Just let it go." But the brothers clung to a single, desperate belief: *There must be a place in this country to reason!* Armed with nothing but this stubborn conviction, they persisted for five agonizing years. The family plunged into desperate poverty and crippling debt. The financial ruin was so total that Liu Fengjin was eventually forced to abandon the village entirely.

Finally, their relentless cries reached the high-level provincial authorities. Shan Rongfan, vice director of the provincial People's Congress, caught wind of the story and ordered a thorough, uncompromising investigation. The Qiqihar City Public Security Bureau was forced to reopen the case, and the ugly truth finally saw the light of day. The Liu brothers were innocent victims who had been maliciously framed and tortured as criminals. Yu Zhongbin, the officer who actually stole the money, had been walking free, while the innocent Liu Fenghai had been unlawfully detained and beaten until he died. From beginning to end, it was a horrifying miscarriage of justice.

In June 1997, Yu Zhongbin was finally arrested for the crime of framing. In January 2000, Director Tian Xiaoming was arrested by the Qiqihar City Procuratorate for suspected dereliction of duty, and his trial opened in April. It was a massive sensation in the village; the local newspaper headlines proudly screamed, "Farmers Bring Down Corrupt Police Director!" 

But what was the final result? 

When the verdict was handed down in May, Tian Xiaoming was indeed found guilty of dereliction of duty. However, "in view of his past contributions to public security work," he was officially exempted from criminal punishment. He was let off the hook with a slap on the wrist. As for the other officers involved—Li Guangcai, Li Changyou, and Li Wenyou—although they were heavily suspected of bending the law for personal gain, their investigations quietly fizzled out, ending in absolutely nothing.

On New Year's Eve that year, the surviving Liu family members knelt on the freezing street and burned paper money for Liu Fenghai. They were finally able to whisper into the smoke, "Brother, we've redressed your grievance." 

But was it truly worth it? A vibrant human life lost, five long years of blood, sweat, and tears, and what they got in return was an official "exemption from punishment" for the man responsible.

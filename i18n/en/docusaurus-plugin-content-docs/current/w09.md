# When Citizens Sued Officials—and Won

*2001*

This case was legendary in our area because it was an exceptionally rare instance where ordinary citizens sued government officials—and actually won. I only learned the full, ugly details from the newspapers long after it was over. But here is the complete story of exactly what happened.

The nightmare started in the spring of 1995, smack in the middle of the busy farming season. Two brothers, Liu Fenghai and Liu Fengqi, had just borrowed 500 yuan from their sister's family. For dirt-poor farmers back then, that was a staggering amount of money. They planned to use the cash to buy premium wheat seeds and fertilizer for their dozen acres of land, desperately hoping to finally turn their luck around after the autumn harvest. Early on the morning of April 16th, the two brothers excitedly squeezed onto a packed public bus heading back from the county town to Yongxing Village. The bus was stuffed shoulder-to-shoulder. Halfway through the ride, a heavy-set ticket conductor pushed his way over to them and aggressively demanded another yuan, claiming they were short on their fare. 

The younger brother, Liu Fengqi, had a fiery, straightforward temper. He instantly shouted back, "The other conductor already agreed on the price! Why are you suddenly jacking it up?" The argument escalated in a flash, and the two men started violently shoving each other in the crowded aisle.

When they finally got off the bus, the older brother, Liu Fenghai, immediately tore into his younger sibling. "It was one single yuan! Was it really worth starting a brawl over? Quickly, check your pockets and make sure our money is still there." 

When they reached inside their jackets, their blood ran cold. The remaining 497 yuan they had tucked inside was gone.

They instantly realized the heavy-set conductor must have picked their pockets during the scuffle. Panicking, they flagged down a passing car and chased the bus down. When they finally caught up to the guy, he stubbornly denied everything. But, growing fearful of the enraged brothers, he tried to quietly settle the matter by offering them some cash. This only threw fuel on the fire. The argument exploded into a chaotic fistfight by the side of the road. Just then, two police officers from the local Henan Township Police Station—Yang Yufeng and Shen Zhilong—happened to drive past. They broke up the fight and threw all three men into the back of their cruiser.

The second they stepped out of the police car at the station, the heavy-set man secretly tossed a wad of cash under the vehicle's tires. But sharp-eyed Liu Fengqi spotted it instantly. They picked up the money and counted it: exactly 497 yuan. Caught red-handed with the stolen cash, it seemed the matter was finally settled. But to everyone's absolute shock, the thief suddenly reached into his pocket and flashed an official badge. His name was Yu Zhongbin, and he was an active-duty police officer with the Traffic Police Brigade of the Keshan County Public Security Bureau.

Officer Yang Yufeng, who had arrested them, was a righteous man. He was utterly disgusted that a fellow cop would commit such a crime, but since the money had been fully recovered, he ultimately let Yu Zhongbin go with a stern warning. The Liu brothers were honest, practical farmers who firmly believed that common people shouldn't pick fights with government officials. Getting their seed money back so they could hurry home to plant their fields was all they cared about. Little did they know, their nightmare was only just beginning.

After Yu Zhongbin returned to his station, rather than feeling any shame, the villain struck first. He marched directly into the office of his superior, Bureau Director Tian Xiaoming, and claimed he never stole a dime. He spun a wild, completely fabricated story, insisting that the Liu brothers had brutally beaten him and framed him for theft. Incredibly, Director Tian didn't bother to investigate or verify a single word. He swallowed the lie whole. He immediately labeled the Liu brothers as dangerous "bus bandits and road bullies" and personally led a heavily armed squad of officers out to their village to arrest them. Even the two honorable cops who had handled the initial incident fairly, Yang Yufeng and Shen Zhilong, were immediately suspended by the Director.

That afternoon, the brothers were violently dragged out of their homes and hauled into the public security bureau. During the brutal interrogation, they repeatedly screamed, "We are the victims! We're the ones who were robbed!" but their pleas were answered only with severe beatings. Rumors eventually leaked out that one officer burned Liu Fengqi repeatedly with lit cigarettes, mocking him: "Burning you is letting you off easy." Liu Fengqi was locked in a filthy cell for over a month. His desperate family had to scrape together another 500 yuan in "ransom money" to finally buy his release.

But the elder brother, Liu Fenghai—a powerfully built farmer who could easily haul hundred-pound sacks of grain on his back—never walked out alive. 

On August 16th, the Liu family received a terse, terrifying notice stating that Liu Fenghai was "sick." Liu Fengqi immediately rushed to the public security bureau, where Director Tian Xiaoming coldly slid a piece of paper across his polished desk. "Your brother assaulted a police officer," Tian said smoothly. "Let's just settle this privately now. Sign this paper, take him home, and we'll compensate you with a little money." 

Liu Fengqi refused to sign a damn thing, demanding to see his brother first. He was led down into a dark, damp room. Reaching out in the gloom, his hands found his brother's freezing cold corpse. The once-strong farmer had been reduced to skin and bones, completely unrecognizable.

The public security bureau simply claimed he had died of an unspecified "illness." They refused to say what the illness was, where he had been treated, or how exactly he died. Even more infuriatingly, without waiting for the family's consent or conducting an autopsy, the police forcibly cremated his body. Whispers quickly spread through the village that Liu Fenghai had been detained far beyond the legal time limit and literally tortured to death. He was only 36 years old, unmarried, and had spent his entire life working the fields to help his younger siblings survive.

With their brother dead and not even his ashes returned to them, the Liu family refused to swallow the injustice. That year, they embarked on a grueling, agonizing crusade for justice. They petitioned the county government, then the city, then the provincial capital, and finally traveled all the way to Beijing. Liu Fengqi and his fourth brother, Liu Fengjin, braved freezing winds and torrential rain, sleeping on the cold floors of railway stations and under highway overpasses, sometimes going days without a single bite of food.

People in their village constantly tried to talk them out of it. "An arm can't twist a thigh," they would sigh. "Just let it go." But the brothers clung to a single, desperate belief: *There must be a place in this country to reason!* Armed with nothing but this stubborn conviction, they persisted for five agonizing years. The family plunged into desperate poverty and crippling debt. The financial ruin was so total that Liu Fengjin was eventually forced to abandon the village entirely.

Finally, their relentless cries reached the highest provincial authorities. Shan Rongfan, vice director of the provincial People's Congress, caught wind of the story and ordered a thorough, uncompromising investigation. The Qiqihar City Public Security Bureau was forced to reopen the case, and the ugly truth finally saw the light of day. The Liu brothers were innocent victims who had been maliciously framed and tortured. Yu Zhongbin, the cop who actually stole the money, had been walking free, while the innocent Liu Fenghai had been unlawfully detained and beaten until he died. From beginning to end, it was a horrifying miscarriage of justice.

In June 1997, Yu Zhongbin was finally arrested for the crime of framing. In January 2000, Director Tian Xiaoming was arrested by the Qiqihar City Procuratorate for suspected dereliction of duty, and his trial opened in April. It was a massive sensation in the village; local newspaper headlines proudly screamed, "Farmers Bring Down Corrupt Police Director!" 

But what was the final result? 

When the verdict came down in May, Tian Xiaoming was indeed found guilty of dereliction of duty. However, "in view of his past contributions to public security work," he was officially exempted from criminal punishment. He was let off the hook with a slap on the wrist. As for the other officers involved—Li Guangcai, Li Changyou, and Li Wenyou—although they were heavily suspected of bending the law for personal gain, their investigations quietly fizzled out, ending in absolutely nothing.

On New Year's Eve that year, the surviving Liu family members knelt on the freezing street and burned paper money for Liu Fenghai. They were finally able to whisper into the smoke, "Brother, we've redressed your grievance." 

But was it truly worth it? A vibrant human life lost, five long years of blood, sweat, and tears, and what they got in return was an official "exemption from punishment" for the man responsible.
